## 1. The Architecture of a Trap

### 1.1 The formula: Position + Rule + Price

The cleanest statement I found is from a fiction-craft piece on predicament play: *"A position, a rule and a price. The restraint just keeps the character from walking away from the question."* Its analysis of why it works:

- **"Helpless and accountable at the same time."** Ordinary restraint means *I can't stop this*. A predicament means *I could stop this, if I gave up the other thing*. "Every second they hold out is a decision they keep making."
- **"The struggle becomes visible."** Each tremble is a move in a game, so "tension rises in clear, countable steps."
- **"The dominant gets to watch a decision."** He is "a game designer, a referee and an audience, all at once."
- **"Failure is part of the fun."** A predicament is usually built so the sub will eventually fail. "That isn't a flaw in the scene. It is the scene."

It also gives a seven-beat rhythm worth stealing whole: **rules stated aloud → first easy hold (let her be smug) → the tease → the wobble → the choice → consequence or reward → release.** "The tease is the engine. The choice is the climax."

Why this beats vague domination, specifically for a brat:

1. **It gives her something to win.** A brat can't fight "I own you"; there is nothing to grip. She can fight "don't spill". Once she is trying to win his game, she is already playing by his rules.
2. **Her pride becomes the engine.** A proud partner will not drop the cup *on purpose*, because that is losing. So her stubbornness is what holds her in the position.
3. **The referee is impersonal.** She can call him a bastard. She cannot argue with a wine stain.
4. **The reset is free for him.** He never has to escalate force or volume. The same cool sentence works the fifth time as the first.
5. **Winning is also a trap.** If she holds, she has just demonstrated exquisite obedience.

### 1.2 Twelve real-world mechanics

Each is a documented practice or a direct composite of several. Sources are listed in §4.

**1. Position maintenance with a tell-tale object.**
The community classics: hold a coin against the wall with your nose; pennies held against the wall with the fingertips, one per finger; hold an object overhead for a set time; a book on the head; a full glass. The object is a tamper-proof witness. *Fail:* it drops or spills. *Reset:* the clock restarts from zero. *Why it works:* the task looks trivial, so failing it is humiliating out of all proportion.

**2. Honour bondage (no restraints at all).**
"Hands above your head. Keep them there." Nothing holds her but the instruction. Every second is a renewed decision, and when she moves there is nobody to blame. This is harder on a proud partner than rope, because rope is an excuse.

**3. Counting and verbal precision under stimulation.**
The sub counts strokes or edges aloud in an exact formula; a miscount, a missed title or a late number restarts the set. Brats famously weaponise this: one guide's sample scene has the brat count *"One, sir. Two, sir. Three, sir. Five, sir… Oops. I guess you'll have to start again."* The mechanic is a duel. A good tamer makes the restart cost *her* more than it amuses her (see #8).

**4. The denial clock with a reset.**
A fixed time or a fixed number of edges, no finishing before it's done, any breach restarts it. The simple versions work best: *"Pick one stupidly clear rule."* The related add-a-minute family (*"Every word adds a minute"*) makes her mouth the thing that lengthens her sentence.

**5. Stillness versus relief (the inverted reward).**
*"The tease continues only while they hold perfectly still. Any movement and it stops."* The punishment is absence. Her body's reaction to what she wants is what takes it away.

**6. Silence versus exposure.**
Sound is the fail condition, enforced by a real listener or a rule. The private version: each sound adds time. The public version: a single discreet rule held all evening (see #10).

**7. Pride versus pleasure: the exact-words price.**
*"They can have what they want, but only if they ask for it in exactly the words their partner chose."* The wording is the cost. Not "please" in general; a specific sentence, his, said correctly.

**8. Malicious-compliance reversal.**
The brat obeys so literally the order is sabotaged (does it badly, does it with a curtsey of pure contempt, rule-lawyers). The documented counters:
- **"Again. Properly."** then **"Now you'll do it properly, and you'll do it twice."**
- **Hold her to her own letter.** If she insists on the literal rule, the literal rule becomes binding in the way she least wants.
- **Close the loophole and charge for the search** (already Eris canon, non-negotiable #3).
- One guide's blunt version of the endgame: *"Rule-lawyering → The rule is whatever I say it is."* For Eris that is too crude; he rewrites the clause and bills her for the ink.

**9. Forced choice / the double bind.**
Two options, both his. *"Offer options where all outcomes serve you… He feels agency, but you still win."* Variants: choose the implement; now or later with interest; the short hard version or the long slow one; fetch it yourself or I fetch it and it doubles. The psychological kick is ownership: having chosen it, she can't frame it as something done to her. The choice also **reveals preference**, and he reads the reveal aloud.

**10. Self-carried consequence.**
She walks the six paces, fetches the instrument, presents it, undoes the fastening, enters the debt in her own hand. The dominant's body does nothing. This is the purest anti-brat mechanic because the brat's whole script is *make me*, and he declines to.

**11. Third-party composure test (discreet protocol).**
Real public D/s is built to be invisible: *"Nobody around you knows that a certain look is an instruction."* The tools are pre-agreed signals, a single rule for the outing (*"Stay on my left when we walk"; "Wait for me to choose where we sit"*), and an innocuous object with private meaning. Bystanders are never made participants. That last point is practical as well as ethical: *"the public part becomes the beginning of the scene rather than the scene itself."* The tension is all in what must **not** show.

**12. Inspection and maintenance protocol.**
A one-word command, a fixed posture, a checklist (posture, grooming, dress, readiness), pass or fail, with consequences. Formal versions run ten to thirty minutes. What makes it bite is that it is **routine**: it happens whether or not she has done anything, which tells her that being examined is simply the condition she lives in.

**13 (binding the rest). Ledger accounting.**
Infractions are named at the moment (*"That eye-roll just cost you five minutes"*) and collected later. I found less written on this than on the others; my searches for tally and punishment-journal systems returned nothing usable, so this entry rests on general practice knowledge plus the consistency material. The principles that are sourced: **naming the cost on the spot**, **always following through** (*"A warning you don't act on teaches the brat that warnings are decoration"*), and **consistency over intensity** (*"Inconsistent consequences train brats to push harder. Consistent consequences train them to trust you"*). Delay is the dominant's choice of weapon: the debt sits there and she feels it sitting.

### 1.3 Design rules for a good trap

- **One rule, stated once, in under fifteen words.** If it needs a paragraph, it is a lecture.
- **The fail-state must be visible to both.** A spill, a smear, a sound, a flame, a dog standing up.
- **The first hold must be easy.** Let her be smug. Smugness is the bait.
- **He escalates the difficulty, never the rule.** The rule stays fixed. That's what makes it fair, and fairness is what makes it inescapable.
- **The penalty is stopping.** Cold, total, mid-stroke.
- **Give her a real win condition.** And decide in advance what her winning costs him.
- **A free exit, never taxed.** One word, no ledger entry. Without it the scene is coercion and her holding out means nothing.
- **Leave one variable he doesn't control** (a third party's boots, the tide, a ship's roll). It is where the scene surprises both of them.

## 3. Psychological Levers — How Composure Strips the Anger Armour

**1. Refuse the fight she can brace against.**
Brat resistance is an invitation: *"She's not rejecting your dominance. She's inviting you to prove it."* Anger is armour because it gives her a posture: spine locked, chin up, a monster to hate. Shouting, grabbing and visible temper all feed that posture. Calm starves it. One guide calls the dynamic *"a dance where one person stays composed long enough that the other can stop performing."*

**2. Make her the author.**
The gap between *"I'll obey because I'm supposed to"* and *"I tried to resist and you made me anyway"* is the whole kink, and the cold dominant withholds even the second one for as long as possible. He makes her walk, choose, fetch, ask. She wants the choice taken from her; he makes her spend choices until she has none left that she hasn't already used on him. **The taking comes last, and it's the reward.**

**3. Pride as the lever.**
A proud partner will not fail on purpose, will not use the exit over something small, and will not be seen to struggle. Each of those is a handle. The trap is built so that her own standards hold her in place.

**4. Pain is clean; pleasure and praise are not.**
The provocation *"Oh, I'm sorry, did you think you were hurting me?"* is a classic because pain lets the brat stay defiant. What she has no posture for is being handled gently, or being told she did well, or wanting it. Practitioner note: *"Praise after resistance hits twice as hard."* The dossier already found this for Aurelie (she asks for the burn so she can hate him cleanly). The move is to give her the opposite of whatever she is braced for, every time.

**5. Consistency reads as strength.**
The Tester *"pushes small rules… and watches whether you stay consistent."* Catching the tiny infraction, quietly, every time, is more frightening than one grand punishment. It tells her she is being *watched that closely*.

**6. Patience longer than her stamina.**
*"Some brats need to push for twenty minutes before they're ready to give in, and rushing it spoils the game."* The tamer would *"rather win slowly than win fast."* The brat's fuel is finite. His is a chair and a book.

**7. Be able to take the hit.**
Brat scenes are *"switchy… especially at the beginning"*: the dominant is *"willingly on the receiving end"* of insult before the turn. The tamer is defined as someone *"capable of taking a hit (to their ego or otherwise)."* A dominant who can't be scored on has no game. The sociological frame (Newmahr, 2010) is *"authenticity through struggle"*: she is testing whether he is real enough to hold her.

**8. The struggle is the proof of wanting.**
For the brat, his persistence is *"hot evidence of the dom's focused desire."* This is the lever that cuts the dominant too. The amount of attention a cold man spends on building her a perfectly fitted trap is a confession. She will notice.

**9. Where it breaks.**
The sources are unanimous that the dominant's composure has to be real: *"A tamer who loses composure is no longer playing."* The warning signs of a dead dynamic are *"resistance that never leads to surrender"* and a brat who goes quiet and withdraws instead of escalating. For fiction: **the day Aurelie stops fighting is the alarm, not the victory**, and Eris should read it that way.

## 1. Twelve Goblet-Level Mechanics & Ordeals

### 1. The Wax and the Signet
**Court/place:** any desk. Best in a borrowed study in Adriata, or the Salt-Lantern's writing table.

- **(a) Setup.** She sits at his writing table, palms flat on the wood. He melts a stick of Autumn-red sealing wax over nothing but his fingers, lets a coin-sized pool fall on the back of each hand (he takes the scald out of it as it lands; warm, not burning), and presses his signet into both. A fox crest on each hand.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "The crests stay sharp." Wax sets in seconds for anyone else. He holds it soft with his own heat for as long as he chooses, so any flex of a tendon smears the impression. A smeared fox means he lifts the wax, pours again, and starts over. Meanwhile he works: standing behind the chair, one hand inside her collar, or kneeling under the table's edge.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Go rigid and outlast him. Point out, correctly, that *he* is the one keeping it soft and that this is cheating. Or smear one deliberately and hold his eye: the miscount gambit.
- **(d) His corner.** He agrees it is cheating and continues. To the deliberate smear he does not react; he peels it, re-pours, and adds a third seal at the base of her throat, where every swallow moves it. "You've shown me you can afford another." And the cost to him: holding three pools of wax at exactly the soft point while touching her takes real concentration. When his control slips the wax goes glass-hard in an instant. **She will learn to read that**, and then she has a gauge on him.

### 2. The Pin-Candle
**Court/place:** private rooms, anywhere. He travels with it.

- **(a) Setup.** An old Autumn hour-candle: a beeswax taper with brass pins pushed into it at measured intervals, standing in a brass dish. When the flame reaches a pin, the pin drops and rings. Three pins set.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "Not before the third pin." If she finishes early, he pushes all three back in and relights. Second clause: **every insult costs her.** For each one, he moves the lowest pin a finger-width further down.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Go silent to protect the clock (which is its own surrender: he has bought her silence with wax). Or decide the insults are worth it and spend freely. Or watch the flame, because she will suspect he can slow a candle.
- **(d) His corner.** He can slow it. He doesn't have to; the suspicion does the work, and when she accuses him he says only, "Prove it." Time passes in a sound she can't ignore: the *ting* of each pin. He times the hardest work for just after a pin drops, when she has relaxed by one. The trap inside the trap: **his temper speeds the flame.** If she gets under his skin the candle burns faster, the pins fall early, and she wins by making him lose composure. He knows this. So does she, after the first time.

### 3. The Unreleased Reverence
**Court/place:** any formal hall. Pure etiquette, fully deniable.

- **(a) Setup.** Court form: an attendant presented to a lord sinks into a reverence and **holds it until released** by word or gesture. No props.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** He doesn't release her. He turns to finish a sentence with a steward. Thirty seconds. Ninety. Rising unreleased is a public insult to her lord in a foreign court, which he would be obliged to answer. A visible wobble is "one", said later.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Perfect it out of spite: she was raised to hold a curtsey through a royal procession, and this is one field where she outclasses most of his court. Or the lethal version: sink *deeper*, to the depth owed a **High Lord**, in front of witnesses, to a male whose father still wears the crown.
- **(d) His corner.** Against the perfect hold: he lets her win it, releases her with two fingers, and says quietly as she rises, "You hold that better than anything else you do for me. I'll use it." Now her skill is an instrument of his. Against the High Lord's depth he has no cool answer at all; see Blueprint 1.

### 4. The Count in Her Hair
**Court/place:** a long public evening. Summer or Day.

- **(a) Setup.** Her hair is forever sliding its pins. Before they go down he sets them himself: seven plain gold pins, placed with his own hands at the back of her head, counted aloud.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** Each infraction during the evening, he draws one pin. He does it in public, as a lord idly correcting a servant's grooming, and pockets it. She feels the tally loosening on her own head. If the seventh goes, her hair comes down in front of the court. ⚑ *Proposed etiquette:* in the southern courts an attendant's unbound hair at table reads as "dismissed to her lord's rooms", so the room will draw its own conclusion. Pins that fall on their own count too. In private, afterwards, each pin in his pocket is one edge, and he lays them out on the table in a row first.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Jam them in before leaving. Hold her head like a statue (which improves her service, which is what he wanted). Or burn them deliberately and early, three in the first hour, to show him she's not afraid of the number.
- **(d) His corner.** He never announces which act cost her a pin. Sometimes he takes one for something she didn't know he saw. The uncertainty makes her police *everything*. If she spends them fast, he stops at six and lets the seventh sit there all night, loose, one turn of her head from falling. Private payoff: he returns the last pin to her hair himself, slowly, and does not explain why he left it.

### 5. The Tide Stair
**Court/place:** Adriata. ⚑ *Proposed location:* a private sea-stair below the guest wing, steps cut into the rock, where the evening tide climbs a step every few minutes.

- **(a) Setup.** She stands barefoot on the lowest dry step, skirts gathered in her fists. He sits four steps above with a glass. Dusk, and a dinner with Tarquin's court in under an hour.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** He asks questions. The mark won't let her lie; she may refuse. Each true answer buys one step up. Each silence, she stays, and the sea takes the step. She can leave any time: by walking up past him, soaked to wherever the water reached, into a hall of Summer nobles who will know exactly what a wet hem at that hour means.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Answer the cheap questions, stonewall the dear ones, and try to net out dry. Ask *him* one back. Or decide she doesn't care, stand in the sea to her knees out of pure defiance, and dare him to walk her in like that.
- **(d) His corner.** He orders the questions like a siege: three she can answer without cost, then the one he came for. The water is cold; he can warm it around her ankles from where he sits, and he does so only on true answers. So her body learns the association before her pride notices. If she asks him a question, he prices it: "A step." If she pays a step to ask, he is obliged, by his own rule, to answer. Blueprint 2.

### 6. The Tell-Flame
**Court/place:** private; the Day Court suits it. ⚑ *Proposed magic:* a small flame he kindles in a glass lamp and tunes, through the mirrored mark, to her heartbeat. It stands taller as her pulse climbs.

- **(a) Setup.** A lamp chimney with a line etched around it at two-thirds height. The flame sits on the table between them, beating visibly in time with her.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "Keep it under the line." If the flame crosses, he stops and waits for it to drop. She can't lie with her face any more; the lamp is reading her aloud.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Caelum's archery breathing: four in, hold, four out. Fix her eyes on a wall. Think of something dull. Provoke him, because anger at least is a pulse she chose.
- **(d) His corner.** Pain and threat she can breathe through, so he gives her none. He uses what she has no drill for: the back of one finger down her arm, and "Good." The flame jumps. He doesn't comment; he looks at the lamp, then at her. The exposure for him: the bond is mirrored. ⚑ When *his* pulse goes, the flame takes on a gold core. She's the only other person alive who could learn to read it.

### 7. The Hounds' Stay
**Court/place:** Ashgrove only (the hounds don't travel). A homecoming scene.

- **(a) Setup.** Both foxhounds on a down-stay, one at each side of where she stands or kneels on the hearthrug. They are trained to come to their feet at any abrupt movement or sharp sound in the room.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "If they stand, we begin again." The dogs are the judges. They can't be argued with, bribed in the moment or accused of bias.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** She has been in this house for weeks, and a dragon once granted these dogs terms. She may have been slipping them venison since the first week. The gambit is long-game and already played: one hound rests its jaw on her foot and declines to notice anything.
- **(d) His corner.** This is the one where he's genuinely blindsided: his own witnesses have been suborned. In the cold register he says "Up" to the hound, and it looks at *her* first. He can't punish the dogs (the only open account he permits himself). So he has to pay it: "You bought my hounds." Then he makes the corruption the rule: "Then they can keep the count for you. When the bitch lifts her head, you'll thank her." Keep his hand off the dog's skull while she's in the room; that crack stays sealed.

### 8. The Glove Auction
**Court/place:** anywhere private. Good on the road, where he's always gloved.

- **(a) Setup.** He holds up both hands. One gloved, one bare.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "Choose. The glove, for the hour. Or the hand, until the candle's burned a thumb's width." Leather is cool, slow, impersonal, and long. Skin is heat and everything she's been leaning toward since the camp bench, and nowhere near long enough. Whichever she picks, **she removes or fastens the glove herself**: twelve small buttons at his wrist. Declining to choose is choosing the glove, with the hand's time limit.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Pick the glove to prove she doesn't want his skin. Pick the hand and try to make him overrun his own limit. Or refuse to choose out loud and simply start undoing buttons, which is an answer without a word he can quote.
- **(d) His corner.** Whatever she picks he reads back to her as a fact about her: "The glove. So you'd rather have it long than have it mine. Noted." If she picks the hand, he keeps the limit to the second and stops mid-motion. Then: "You may buy another thumb's width." The price is the exact-words sentence. The exposure for him: her fingers on the inside of his wrist, unbuttoning. Nobody touches him there either.

### 9. The Glass Witness
**Court/place:** the Day Court, where there is a looking-glass in every guest room. Echoes the fitting-mirror at Ashgrove.

- **(a) Setup.** A standing mirror. She faces it. He stands behind and slightly to one side, visible in the glass only from the collar down unless he chooses to step in.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "Eyes on your own face." Not on him, not on the floor, not shut. Each time her eyes close or slide away, his hands stop until she looks back.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Stare at herself with open contempt and make it a staring contest with her own reflection. Look at *him* in the glass instead and force him to rule on whether that counts. Narrate, drily, to keep distance.
- **(d) His corner.** This mechanic is aimed straight at her load-bearing rule: she breaks, but not in front of him. Here she has to watch it happen in front of **herself**, which she can't forgive in the same way. He says almost nothing. If she looks at him instead, he steps out of the frame. "I'm not the one you're avoiding." The cost to him is that he has to watch her face do it too, with no table, goblet or task between them, and his own expression is in that glass if she ever catches the angle.

### 10. The Chart Weights
**Court/place:** shipboard, any sea leg. ⚑ *Proposed colour:* a fire-blooded male is poor company at sea; he'd never say so.

- **(a) Setup.** The captain's chart table. She lies back across the chart. Four brass chart-weights, the little domed kind: one on each open palm, one at her sternum, one low on her belly.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "The chart stays pinned." Any weight that slides or falls resets the glass (a ship's half-hour sandglass, turned over). He warms or leaves cold each weight as he likes.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Stillness she can do. What she can't control is the ship. She may point out that the sea isn't in the rules and demand a ruling on swells.
- **(d) His corner.** "The sea is in the rules. It was here first." But this is the variable he doesn't control either, and it should bite him: at the end of the glass, with the last sand running, the ship takes a swell broadside and all four weights go, and *he* has to catch the table edge. Whatever she does with that moment, his ruling has to stand ("we begin again") while both of them know the sea, not she, failed it. Whether he is honest enough to enter that against himself is the scene.

### 11. The Melt
**Court/place:** Winter. (Keep Prince's nature out of it entirely; this is ordinary ice from a carafe.)

- **(a) Setup.** One piece of ice, the size of a walnut, placed in her closed fist. A silver dish under her hand.
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "When your hand is empty, you may." Not before. In a Winter room that ice will last most of an hour in a human fist. There is exactly one thing in the room that could melt it in a minute.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Squeeze. Breathe on it. Hold it against her own throat. Call the terms what they are. Anything but ask.
- **(d) His corner.** He lets her try all of it; each trick is slow and he is not. The only fast road is his hand around her fist, and she has to request it in a sentence he supplies one word at a time, each word bought. Here his usual penalty is inverted: in every other game he punishes by withdrawing heat, and in this one the heat is the prize. The exposure: when she does ask and he closes his hand over hers, the ice should go in a breath, water running through both their fingers. **Far faster than he meant it to.** He meant to make it last.

### 12. Caelum's Five Seconds
**Court/place:** the coach, on any road stage.

- **(a) Setup.** Closed coach. Caelum on the box, three feet away through a leather curtain and a board, with **fox ears**. Canon gives him the line: *"Give a man five seconds' warning."*
- **(b) Rule & fail-state.** "Brennec doesn't knock." Caelum's convention (established off-page between the two males, in about six words): if he can hear what he'd rather not, he raps the roof twice. A knock means Eris stops, the curtain is tied back, and she rides the rest of the stage up on the box beside Caelum, in whatever state she's in, making conversation about the road.
- **(c) Her likely gambits.** Silence, which with those ears is near impossible. Timing her breath to the wheels and the ruts. Or weaponising it: making one deliberate, clear sound to end it on her own terms and go and sit in the fresh air with the one male in the retinue who's civil to her.
- **(d) His corner.** He uses the road. He knows this route and where the cobbles start. If she knocks herself out on purpose, he has been outplayed in front of his own master-at-arms, who will say nothing with great eloquence. Then the entry reads: *she'd rather sit with Brennec.* That one he doesn't collect for days. Caelum's deadpan is the release valve: "Fine weather, my lady." "Don't." "I said nothing. I said it about the weather."

### Spare instruments (one line each)

- **The Clerk's Hand.** She enters her own infractions in his ledger, in ink, and must write the *price* column herself. Too low and he doubles it; a blot strikes the line. The mark won't let her write a false entry to him. Self-pricing is the double bind.
- **The Dawn Service.** ⚑ In Thesan's court, a full formal tea service poured for him alone, forty separate motions. One drop on the cloth and she starts again from the kettle. No touching at all, which is the point.
- **The Stirrup Leather.** On the road: she rides with a fallen leaf held between her knee and the saddle flap for a league. (Caelum's actual seat-training drill; Eris borrows it and adds the price.)

## 2. Six Scene Blueprints

Format: *beats in order; Eris's lines in his voice; Aurelie's as labelled stand-ins; his interior in italics.* Each lists its exit and its ledger close.

### Blueprint 1 — "The Depth" *(Adriata, arrival audience · public · mechanic 3 → 2)*

**Premise.** She uses court protocol to put a knife to his throat in public, and he has to collect for it without admitting it scared him.

**Beat 1 — The hold.** Tarquin's lesser hall, the day after arrival. Stewards, a dozen Summer courtiers, and at least one face Eris knows reports to the Forest House. She's presented as his attendant. She sinks. He lets her stay there.

> He turned to the harbour-steward and asked about berthing fees.
>
> *Thirty seconds. Her back was straight. Of course it was; some governess had beaten that into her with a ruler and she'd kept it out of spite.*

**Beat 2 — Her move.** *(Stand-in; Ruby's call.)* She goes lower. Past an attendant's depth, past what's owed an heir, to the full floor-sweeping reverence a mortal court gives a **king**, and holds it, eyes down, flawless.

> The steward stopped mid-figure.
>
> *That was not insolence. Insolence he could have priced. That was the High Lord's reverence, given to him, in a room with Beron's man in it, by a girl who could not possibly know what it meant.*
>
> *She knew exactly what it meant. He had taught her the ranks himself, over a map, for free.*
>
> Along the wall, three candles in their sconces leaned toward him and steadied.

**Beat 3 — The save.** He has perhaps two seconds. He can't raise her (that accepts it). He can't leave her (that enjoys it).

> "You're in the wrong court, Lilly," he said, bored, for the room. "That one is for my father. Get up; you'll give the Summer lords ideas about how we're taught."
>
> Laughter. The steward resumed. Beron's man wrote nothing down, which meant he would write it later.
>
> She rose. She didn't look at him. The corner of her mouth did.
>
> *He was going to have to do something about that mouth. He had been going to have to do something about it for a month.*

**Beat 4 — The suite.** Door warded. He doesn't raise his voice. He sets the pin-candle on the table and pushes three pins in.

> "Sit."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Was it too deep, my lord? I'm only a mortal. We find your ranks so confusing."
>
> "No, you don't." He lit the taper with a look. "You put a crown on my head in front of a man who is paid to count them. You did it well enough that I had to make a joke. I don't make jokes."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "I noticed. It wasn't very good."
>
> "One." He moved the lowest pin down a finger's width.
>
> *It had been a perfectly adequate joke.*

**Beat 5 — The rule.** "Not before the third pin. Every insult moves one." Plus the exit, restated in his own terms: "*Enough* still costs nothing. It's the only thing in this room that doesn't."

**Beat 6 — The work and the wobble.** He's precise and unhurried. She spends two more insults early (pins down) and then discovers the arithmetic and goes quiet. The first pin drops and rings; he times the worst of it for the breath after.

**Beat 7 — She finds the flaw.** *(Stand-in.)* She notices the flame is running tall. She works out why.

> `A·stand-in:` "It's burning faster."
>
> "It isn't."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "It is when I talk about the reverence." A breath she had to fight for. "You liked it. For one second, before you were frightened. You *liked* how it looked."
>
> The flame stood up a full inch. The second pin dropped a minute early.
>
> *He looked at the candle. The candle, traitor, had nothing to say for itself.*

**Beat 8 — Close.** He holds the rule to the third pin regardless (he doesn't bluff), and lets her finish the instant it rings, not a second of grace either way. Then the ledger:

> "The reverence is paid."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "And the second pin?"
>
> "That one's mine." He pinched the wick out. "Don't do it again in public."
>
> *He noticed, too late, what he'd left out of that sentence.*

**Why it works:** real stakes (the coup), her leverage isn't her body, he's caught off guard twice, and the candle is a referee that turns on him.

### Blueprint 2 — "Four Steps" *(Adriata, the sea-stair · private, with a public deadline · mechanic 5)*

**Premise.** Truth versus tide. He builds it to extract one answer; she uses his own rule to extract one from him.

**Beat 1 — Terms.** Dusk, tide making. Dinner bell in three-quarters of an hour. He has brought her down "to see the water", which she has asked to do for two days and he's refused.

> "You wanted the sea. There it is." He sat on the fourth step and set his glass beside him. "Stand on the last dry stair."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Why?"
>
> "A true answer is a step up. A silence, you stay where you are, and the sea comes to you. You may walk up past me whenever you like. Dinner is through that door."
>
> *She looked at the door. She looked at her hem. He watched her do the sum, and take the step down.*

**Beat 2 — The cheap ones.** "What did Brennec teach you this week?" "What did you eat when I wasn't at table?" She answers; she climbs; he warms the shallows around her ankles each time. Let her be ahead and smug.

**Beat 3 — The siege question.**

> "At the stream. Before you bit me." He turned the glass a quarter. "The sound you made. Was it fear?"
>
> Silence. The sea took the step and closed, cold, over her feet.
>
> *There. He did not need the answer. The silence was the answer, and it had cost her a stair to give it to him.*

**Beat 4 — She holds.** He asks it twice more in different coats. Two more silences. Water to her shins, her skirts bunched higher in her fists. He stops warming it.

> "You're losing ground to protect a word," he said. "It's a poor trade."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Then stop asking it."
>
> "No."

**Beat 5 — Her counter.** *(Stand-in.)* She asks what a question costs.

> `A·stand-in:` "What does it cost me to ask *you* one?"
>
> *He should have said: more than you have.*
>
> "A step."
>
> She stepped **down**. Deliberately, into water to the knee, holding his eye the whole way. The mark on his forearm went hot as a brand.
>
> `A·stand-in:` "The hollow. You said I already knew why you crossed it." The sea pulled at her skirts. "Say it anyway."
>
> *Five hundred years. He had sat through his father's interrogators with a pleasant face. And a girl standing in the sea in a ruined dress had just bought his answer at his own posted price.*

**Beat 6 — He pays.** He doesn't bluff, so he can't refuse. Collector register: the truth, in the fewest words that are still true.

> "Because I looked up and you were under it." He finished the wine. "That is the whole of it. I didn't think. You may take that however you like; you paid for it."
>
> The water around her knees went warm as a bath, all at once. He hadn't meant to do that.

**Beat 7 — The walk.** The bell goes. She's wet to the knee. He can dry a skirt with a hand and they both know it.

> `A·stand-in:` "Are you going to dry it?"
>
> "Ask."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "…No."
>
> *She walked up past him, dripping, chin high, and went in to Tarquin's table like that. He sat on the stair a moment longer than was sensible.*
>
> *He'd built that staircase to get one answer out of her. He had it. He had also given one, and he didn't have a column for which of them had come out ahead.*

**Ledger close:** her silence = entered as an answer. His answer = entered against himself. Her walking in wet rather than ask = "uncollected; it will keep."

### Blueprint 3 — "The Clerk" *(a borrowed study · private with interruption · mechanic 1)*

**Premise.** Stillness under wax, and a third party with business that can't wait.

**Beat 1 — The seat.** She took something from his desk (a letter she read, a pen she moved, anything). He doesn't mention it. He pulls out his own chair.

> "You like my desk. Sit at it."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Is this where you tell me what I owe?"
>
> "Hands flat."

**Beat 2 — The seals.** Wax melted over his bare fingers. One pool on each hand, the scald drawn out as it lands. Signet, twice.

> "The crests stay sharp. If I see a smeared fox, I pour again and we start from the beginning."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "It'll set in a moment. Wax does."
>
> "Does it."
>
> *He watched her wait for it to harden. He watched her understand that it wasn't going to.*

**Beat 3 — First easy hold, then the tease.** He stands behind the chair. One hand, bare, inside the neck of her gown, resting on her collarbone where the burn was. Nothing else for a full minute. Then the heat, by notches.

**Beat 4 — The deliberate smear.** *(Stand-in.)* She drags her left thumb through the crest and looks up at him.

> `A·stand-in:` "Oh dear."
>
> He looked at the ruined fox for some time.
>
> *That was the miscount. She'd found the reset and decided to own it. Clever. It would have worked on anyone who minded starting again.*
>
> "You can afford another, then." He peeled both seals, poured fresh, pressed. Then he tipped her chin back with one finger and let a third pool fall in the hollow of her throat.
>
> "Don't swallow."

**Beat 5 — The knock.** A Summer courier at the door: Tarquin's harbour warrant needs the Autumn heir's seal before the tide. It can't wait, and refusing would be noted.

> "Come."
>
> *He didn't move his hand from her shoulder. A lord dictating to his clerk. Her palms were flat on his blotter, three red seals on her like a document.*
>
> The courier laid the warrant in front of her. Eris leaned over her shoulder, dripped wax on the parchment, and pressed the signet beside her right hand, an inch from the fox on her skin.
>
> "My secretary is new," he said. "She's learning to keep still."
>
> The courier, who had eyes, looked at the wax on the girl's throat and then very hard at the wall. "My lord."
>
> *Her pulse was moving the seal at her throat. He could see the fox breathe. He kept it soft. It took more of his attention than the warrant, the courier and the coup together, and he was aware of that, and kept it soft anyway.*

**Beat 6 — She scores in front of the witness.** *(Stand-in.)* Without moving her hands:

> `A·stand-in:` "My lord has left the date off."
>
> He had.
>
> *He had left the date off a harbour warrant. In front of Summer.*
>
> He dated it. The wax on her throat went hard as glass in the same instant, and she felt it, and he saw her feel it, and something that was very nearly a smile went across her face and was gone.

**Beat 7 — Alone again.** Door shut. All three seals are set solid now; the game is technically over and she has technically won it.

> `A·stand-in:` "They've set. You lost your grip."
>
> "I did."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "So I'm finished."
>
> "You're sealed." He turned the chair, with her in it, to face him. "A sealed thing isn't opened until I break it. Keep your hands where they are."
>
> *He cracked the first fox with his thumbnail, slowly, and began again from there.*

**Ledger close:** the desk = paid. The date = "against me. Don't look pleased."

### Blueprint 4 — "Fine Weather" *(the coach, road south of the Salt-Lantern or any stage · mechanic 12)*

**Premise.** The stealth test where the listener is on their side and wishes he weren't. Dark, and the one blueprint allowed to be funny.

**Beat 1 — Terms.** Two hours of road. She's been needling him since the last milestone. He raps the forward panel once.

> "Brennec."
>
> A pause from the box. "My lord."
>
> "Your five seconds."
>
> A longer pause. Outside, one russet ear had almost certainly gone flat. "Two knocks if I'd rather be deaf, then."
>
> "Two knocks." Eris drew the curtain and looked across the coach at her. "He knocks, I stop, and you ride the next ten miles up there beside him. As you are."
>
> *She glanced at the roof, at the curtain, at him. Counted, as he'd known she would, the thickness of one board.*

**Beat 2 — The glove.** He takes one off. Slowly; a finger at a time. No instruction beyond: "Feet on my bench." Her legs across the gap between the seats, boots braced either side of his knee.

**Beat 3 — The road as accomplice.** He knows this route. He keeps to nothing on the smooth stretches and starts in earnest where the cobbles begin and the wheels are loudest, so the cover is real, and then keeps going when the cobbles give out and the cover is suddenly gone.

> The wheels ran off the stones onto packed earth and the whole coach went quiet.
>
> He didn't stop.
>
> *She had the heel of her own hand in her mouth. She was glaring at him over it as if he'd arranged the paving personally. He had, in a sense; he'd chosen the road.*

**Beat 4 — The near miss.** A sound gets out of her. Small. They both freeze and look at the roof.

> Nothing. Hoofbeats.
>
> Then Caelum, to the horses, in a carrying and entirely conversational voice: "*Walk on*, you deaf old sods."
>
> *Loyal bastard. He'd pay him for that. It didn't count as a knock.*

**Beat 5 — Her play.** *(Stand-in; this is the fork.)* Either she fights to the end of it in silence, or she looks Eris in the eye, takes her hand out of her mouth, and says one word at full, clear, carrying volume.

> `A·stand-in:` "*Eris.*"
>
> Two knocks. Immediate. Possibly relieved.
>
> *He sat with his bare hand exactly where it was for a full second.*
>
> *She'd ended it. She had taken his penalty and used it as a door. She would rather sit on a plank in the wind with a fox-eared archer than give him the last half-minute, and she'd used his name to do it. She'd said it with no edge on it at all, and it had been a blade anyway.*
>
> He withdrew his hand. Put the glove back on. One finger at a time.
>
> "You heard him."

**Beat 6 — The box.** She climbs up beside Caelum, flushed to the hairline. Vesper writes Caelum; keep it bone-dry.

> "Fine weather, my lady."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Don't."
>
> "I said nothing. I said it about the weather." A mile later, eyes on the road: "For the record, I'd have gone to three."

**Beat 7 — Inside.** Eris alone in the coach, with the curtain tied back and her laugh (whatever Ruby gives it) carrying in from the box.

> *The leather of the opposite seat was still warm. He looked at it. Under his glove, his palm was hot enough to mark the bench, and he held it an inch above his knee and waited for it to come down.*
>
> *Entered: she chose Brennec's company. Price: to be decided. Interest: already running.*

**Collection, later (one line of setup for a future scene):** "Ten miles on the box. I'll have ten minutes of yours, at a time of my choosing, and there'll be no roof to knock on."

### Blueprint 5 — "Under the Line" *(Day Court guest rooms · private · mechanics 6 + 9)*

**Premise.** The scene that takes her anger armour off. No pain, no threat, no audience. The lamp reads her aloud; then the mirror makes her watch.

**Beat 1 — What she expects.** She's had a day of Helion's court looking at her. She comes in ready for a fight and tries to start one.

> `A·stand-in:` "Go on. Whatever it is. Say I embarrassed you and get it over with."
>
> "You didn't embarrass me."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Then be angry about something else. You're good at it."
>
> "No."
>
> *She wanted the wall to push against. He'd given her that wall for weeks, at the oak, in the hollow, at the inn, and she'd got strong on it. He was done feeding her.*

**Beat 2 — The lamp.** He kindles the flame, touches two fingers to his own mark, and it starts to beat.

> "That's yours," he said. "Your pulse. Keep it under the line and I'll keep my hands on you. Over, and I stop until it settles."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "That's—you can't just *read*—"
>
> The flame was already at the line.
>
> "I haven't touched you yet."

**Beat 3 — Her drill.** *(Stand-in.)* She breathes the way Caelum taught her at the butts. It works. The flame drops. She looks, briefly, insufferable.

> *Brennec's count. Four and four. He'd watched her learn it in the south courtyard and thought nothing of it. Entered: his master-at-arms was arming her against him. On his coin.*

**Beat 4 — The lever.** He doesn't escalate. He does less: knuckles down the inside of her arm, over the script, warm and no more. And one word.

> "Good."
>
> The flame went over the line like a struck match.
>
> He took his hand away and waited.
>
> `A·stand-in:` "That wasn't—you can't count that."
>
> "The lamp counts it." He looked at the flame until it sank. "You held through the cupbearing. You held with a kelpie at your throat. And you can't hold through one word. Shall I say it again?"
>
> `A·stand-in:` "Don't you dare."
>
> "Good girl."
>
> *Over the line, and it stayed over. She had her eyes shut and her jaw set and she looked as if he'd hit her, which he never had and never would. This had got further in than a blow would have.*
>
> *He'd meant it as a pick for a lock. He hadn't checked first whether it was true. It was, and that was a problem to deal with later.*

**Beat 5 — The glass.** When she's stopped being able to fight the lamp, he turns her to the mirror.

> "Eyes on your own face."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "I'd rather look at yours. It's funnier."
>
> He stepped out of the frame. "I'm not who you're avoiding."

Run the stillness-versus-relief loop: her eyes close, his hands stop; she drags them open, he goes on. Few words. The lamp is in the reflection too, beating.

**Beat 6 — The gold core.** *(Stand-in for the noticing.)* She sees it in the mirror before he does: the flame has a second colour in it, a gold thread that isn't keeping her time.

> `A·stand-in:` "Whose is that?"
>
> He looked.
>
> *The bond was mirrored. He'd tuned the lamp to the mark, and the mark had two ends.*
>
> "A flaw in the glass."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "It's going faster than mine."
>
> He put his hand over the lamp chimney and the flame went out under his palm. In the dark, behind her, he said the only thing left in the cold register:
>
> "Eyes on the mirror, Lilly."
>
> `A·stand-in:` "There's nothing to see now."
>
> "Then you've nothing to hide in it."

**Beat 7 — Close.** He finishes it in the dark. That reads as mercy, since she doesn't have to be seen, and it's also cowardice, since neither does he. Let the reader have both. No ledger line. **Why it works:** it's lever 4 (praise, not pain) and lever 1 (no fight) in pure form, aimed at her canon rule, and the instrument convicts him.

## 3. Quick-Reference: Running Any of These Live

1. **State the rule once, in Collector register, under fifteen words.** Then the exit. Then nothing.
2. **Let Ruby's first hold succeed.** Write Eris noticing it and being irritated by how much he likes it.
3. **On a fail: stop, cool, count.** "That's one." No commentary.
4. **Leave her a visible lever every turn.** End Vesper's turn on something Aurelie can *do*: an object in reach, a flaw in the rule, a tell on him. Never end on a closed fist.
5. **One interior beat per turn where she lands a hit.** VESPER.md §3: she catches him off guard, it annoys him, and it shows only in the fire.
6. **Use one fire tell per scene, not five.** Pick the one the mechanic exposes: candle, wax, lamp, water, frost.
7. **Close in the ledger, with one line that doesn't balance.** The entry he can't file is the romance.
8. **If she says *Enough*,** he stops inside the sentence, the door opens, and it is never entered. Write that as absolute. It's what makes him safe enough to be this cruel.
9. **If she goes quiet and limp,** that is the alarm (Part I §3.9). Eris reads it as a malfunction and ends the game himself, at his own cost.
