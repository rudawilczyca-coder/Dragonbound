# Dragonbound — Live Session: The Seventh Evening (Lucien's Dinner)
*Started: 1 November 2026*
*Setting: Ashgrove (Eris's private hunting lodge, Autumn Court) — One week after the return from the Forest House*

---

## Turn 1 — Vesper (Eris)

Seven days at Ashgrove did not soften the house; they only taught the stone who lived inside it.

By the seventh afternoon, the mortal dresses from Hightower were gone from the cedar wardrobe in the second-floor bedchamber. In their place hung the seamstress's work—six gowns cut to the severe, high-waisted silhouette of the eastern Autumn marches, their skirts split subtly at the side seam so a woman could pivot on wet flagstone without catching her heel. There was no yellow among them, no pale continental pastel, no lace at the throat. Only heavy forest-green velvet that drank the candlelight, dark tarnished gold the color of old coin, and a winter-weight wool the exact shade of arterial blood spilled on fresh snow.

Down on the south stones, the week had left its own ledger. Caelum Brennec did not praise, and he did not coddle; when an elbow dropped or a heel crossed too narrow, the flat of his ashwood stave tapped the offending joint until the correction became muscle memory. Yet by the sixth dawn, the fox-touched instructor had stopped calling out basic footwork altogether, trading three-beat parries with a quiet, crooked half-nod that—according to Tomas in the stables—was the closest Caelum had come to applauding anyone in forty years. And above the yard, wherever Prince chose to perch or circle, the bond between dragon and rider had thickened from a raw nerve into a cold, faceted wall of ice-bright glass that held steady even when Eris leaned against the terrace balustrade and pressed a deliberate thumb of amber heat against its outer seam.

They had scarcely spoken ten words to each other off the stones all week. Every morning at seven, Eris stood on the flagstones until the red-gold bracelets were unclasped for the drill; every morning at eight, he watched them click shut again before vanishing into his study with couriers' dispatches and wax seals.

Until dusk on the seventh day.

Rain had blown through the copper-leaved oaks an hour before sunset, leaving the courtyard gravel black and gleaming and the air thick with wet bark, woodsmoke, and roasted chestnuts from Briar's ovens. Down in the dining room, the long oak table had been set with three places—not two—the heavy silver polished until it threw back the hearth-fire like blades.

Then the outer perimeter ward at the south gate chimed.

It did not ring once, as it did for a lone rider.

It rang *twice*.

Through the tall casement window of the upper corridor, two horses emerged from the dripping tunnel of red-and-gold trees at a brisk trot. The first rider was unmistakable even through the autumn mist: broad-shouldered in a mud-spattered traveling coat, bright copper hair tied back at the nape, the dull bronze curve of a mechanical eye catching the torchlight as he glanced up at the lodge's roofline.

Beside Lucien rode a second man on a heavy bay stallion—tall, dark-haired, built like an old campaign sword that had survived too many wars, with the unmistakable posture of someone who had never bowed to a High Fae court in his life and had no intention of starting tonight.

Down in the entry hall, boots struck the flagstones as Caius moved to open the great oak doors.

Upstairs, three paces from Aurelie's bedchamber door, the air warmed by five degrees before a single, unhurried knuckle rapped against the dark wood.

Eris did not wait for permission to turn the brass handle. He stepped just inside the threshold and closed the door behind him with the quiet click of a gun cocking.

He was dressed for the table in a tailored coat of black-cherry brocade threaded with dark copper, the high collar framing his throat, his hair loose and gleaming over his shoulders. For a second, his amber eyes swept the room—checking the shadow above the wardrobe rafters, the balcony latch, the red-gold faebane cuffs—before settling on her.

"My brother has a habit of collecting strays," Eris said, his voice a low, even purr designed not to carry past the heavy timber of the door. "It appears he has brought Lord Jurian inside my gates without asking."

He took two slow steps into the room, stopping close enough that the scent of smoke, bitter almond, and rain-cooled autumn air rolled off his coat. His gaze dropped from her face to the cut of the new gown, lingering for a fraction of a second too long on the line of her throat where no mortal lace hid the pulse beneath, before rising back to her eyes.

"A mortal general who spent five centuries in Amarantha's court, and the last two years dealing with every human kingdom across the sea," Eris murmured, tilting his head a fraction. "Which means unlike Lucien, he knows Continental bearing when he sees it—and he knows the cadence of a Hightower noblewoman's tongue."

His right hand rose—not touching her yet, hovering a bare inch from the curve of her jaw, the heat of his skin radiating against hers like an open hearth door.

"Is the dragon tucked behind the glass, Lilly?" he asked softly, the question a blade wrapped in velvet. "Because in three minutes, you are walking down those stairs beside me—and if Jurian smells either a princess or a wyrm in this house, tonight becomes considerably more expensive for us both."

## Turn 2 — Ruby (Aurelie & Prince)

Aurelie didn't like the dress. Not because it was ugly — to the contrary, the velvet was finer than anything she ever wore and the needlework on the small golden leaves was so fine that it almost looked like they were painted rather than sewn in. But when she looked at herself in the mirror, she couldn't ignore the three big issues. 
First was the low cut neckline — or lack thereof — that exposed her entire throat and front almost down to her breasts. She wasn't given any lace, or even jewellery that could take the attention off, so her every swallow was visible. 
Second was the cut in the side, which allowed for a much wider range of movement than the dresses she was accustomed to wearing, but came at the cost of exposing her left leg almost up to the thigh.
Third was the fact that even though the dress was wide and layered, the middle hugged her frame tightly, exposing every curve in her waist.
Aurelie looked away from the mirror when Eris came inside. She didn't dare move away when his hand rose — and then stopped before touching her. 
Something flashed in her eyes at the description of that Lord Jurian, but she quickly hid it away. 
She raised both her hands in front of him, letting the light glint of her faebane bracelets. The bargain mark was also exposed, its lines seemingly having their own golden glow this close to him.
"He is away, my Lord. Hidden in the forest, unless you decide you'd like to present him after all." Her voice was steady, even though there was a tight knot in her stomach for the last two hours. It was one thing, teasing Eris about meeting his brother. Eris, at least, she knew. She had no idea what kind of man his brother — or that Lord Jurian — was.

## Turn 3 — Vesper (Eris, Lucien & Jurian)

Eris did not miss the fraction of a second where her blue eyes sparked at Jurian's name, nor the speed with which she shuttered it behind her lashes.

His hovering hand closed the final inch.

His thumb and forefinger caught her by the chin—not rough enough to bruise, but firm, inescapable, tilting her face up so she could not drop her gaze to the carpet or the mirror. His skin was scorching against hers, a slow, dry heat that made the golden lines of the bargain mark along her raised forearm pulse once in answer, bright as ember-thread beneath her pale skin.

"I saw that little spark behind your eyes, *Lady Aurelie*," he murmured, his voice dropping so low it brushed her bare throat like smoke. His thumb shifted half an inch downward, pressing lightly against the hollow of her throat where her pulse beat uncovered—no lace, no pearls, nowhere to hide the next swallow. "Put it out. Jurian is not a courier to Hightower, and he is not a gallant knight looking for a damsel to smuggle back across the sea. He is a creature who survived five hundred years as an eye and a bone on a tyrant's finger by trading every softer instinct he ever owned. If he suspects what you are worth to Queen Ismena—or what sleeps in my woods—he will sell you before the soup cools."

His gaze dropped to her raised wrists, where the red-gold faebane cuffs caught the candlelight above the glowing gold filigree of his mark, then trailed slowly down the tight, merciless cut of the forest-green velvet at her waist and the flash of pale skin at the slit of her skirt. A slow, dangerous satisfaction flickered in the molten amber of his eyes.

"The seamstress followed her instructions admirably," he said, releasing her chin only to let the back of his knuckles graze the bare skin above her collarbone before his hand fell to his side. "Let him stay in the trees until the port is poured. Watch my hand on the stem of my glass. Until then—you are Lilly. You are human, you are under my roof by a bargain you had the poor judgment to strike, and you speak when spoken to."

He offered his left arm, the black-cherry brocade warm as hearth-stone beneath her fingers, and opened the bedchamber door.

Down the wide timber staircase they went, the rush of rain and voices rising from the lower hall with every step.

In the entry foyer below, Caius was just taking two dripping riding cloaks. Standing near the hearth of the open dining room archway were two men.

Lucien turned first at the sound of the velvet hem whispering down the oak stairs.

Even in mud-spattered leather, the family resemblance was a physical blow—the same lean, aristocratic Vanserra bone structure, the same fiery cascade of copper-red hair—except where Eris was all banked, untouchable ice-and-amber precision, Lucien looked lived-in and weather-beaten. A jagged, pale scar clove from his left brow down across his cheek to his jaw, framing the whirring bronze-and-gold sphere of his mechanical eye.

As Aurelie came into view on Eris's arm, Lucien's mechanical eye gave an audible, tiny *click-whir*, the bronze aperture narrowing on the red-gold faebane cuffs around her wrists, the glowing golden bargain mark climbing her bare forearm, and the severe, revealing cut of Autumn velvet on a nineteen-year-old human girl. Something sharp and overtly protective tightened around Lucien's mouth; his russet-gold eye flashed immediately up to Eris's face in silent, disgusted accusation.

Beside him, Lord Jurian did not look at Eris at all.

The mortal general stood with one mud-stained boot braced near the fender of the hearth, a glass of dark amber whiskey already in his ungloved hand. He was tall and broad-shouldered, his damp dark hair pushed back from a square, stubbled jaw, his rounded human ears almost jarring in a house of High Fae. His dark brown eyes started at the crown of Aurelie's cool chestnut hair, dropped to the bare pulse at her throat, clocked the upright, unbowed line of her spine despite the shackles on her wrists, and paused—for one razor-sharp beat—on the way her chin lifted as she reached the bottom step.

A slow, knowing half-smirk curved Jurian's mouth.

"Well," Jurian drawled, his voice a rich, gravelly baritone honed on battlefields and courtly drawing rooms alike. He raised his glass an inch toward Eris. "Lucien told me on the road that the rumors had to be tavern rot. That even Eris Vanserra wouldn't drag a mortal girl across the Autumn border and lock her in his hunting lodge." His dark eyes slid back to Aurelie, sharp as a scalpel. "Though looking at the way she holds her head on your stairs, Vanserra... I'd wager you didn't pluck this one out of a border village."

"Lord Jurian," Eris said smoothly, his hand resting lightly—and unmistakably—over Aurelie's fingers where they lay on his sleeve, a silent brand of ownership in front of both men. "No one invited you through my gates, and yet here you stand dripping rain on my flagstones. Do try not to speculate on my household before you've earned your supper."

Lucien stepped forward, ignoring Eris entirely, and inclined his head to Aurelie—not the mocking bow of a High Fae lord to a pet, but the measured, respectful nod he would have given a lady in the human courts.

"My lady," Lucien said, his voice warm and rough-edged, both eyes—flesh and bronze—meeting hers squarely. "I am Lucien. And if my brother is half the host he claims to be, you'll allow me to apologize for the hour and the weather."

## Turn 4 — Ruby (Aurelie & Prince)

*'You are human, you are under my roof by a bargain you had the poor judgment to strike, and you speak when spoken to.'*  
Aurelie repeated those sentences to herself like a mantra, as they descended the stairs, her hand resting in the crook of Eris's arm.   
*'You are human, you are under my roof by a bargain you had the poor judgment to strike, and you speak when spoken to.'*  
She repeated in her head, when the two men came into her view. She didn't lower her eyes, and looked straight at them, assessing them just as much as they did her. Jurian was something more familiar. She saw men like him in her life, both visiting her father's house and during the balls and functions she attended. The sight of his human ears felt calming, even despite Eris's warning. Then she looked at Lucien, and she couldn't entirely hide the widening of her eyes when she saw his face. The scar, she could bear. What took her completely off-guard was the eye. And not because she found it grotesque or appalling. The first thought in her head was "How does it work?" and then "Is that magic, technology or both?"   
She wasn't sure if Lucien noticed that, but he spoke to her directly, and so — by Eris's ruling — she was allowed to reply. She smiled gently, the smile that was trained into her by her mother and her governesses.  
"I accept your apology for the hour. As for the weather, forgive me the assumption, but I was under the impression that controlling the weather is not part of the Vanserra heritage. And even if the weather is your doing, sir, I don't mind. I quite like the rain."

## Turn 5 — Vesper (Eris, Lucien & Jurian)

For half a heartbeat, the foyer went dead silent save for the crackle of birch logs in the dining room grate.

Lucien caught the widening of her eyes first. He had braced for it—the tiny, reflexive flinch he got from half the ladies in Prythian and nearly every mortal in the human lands when the bronze orb whirred in its scarred socket. Except she hadn't flinched back. She had leaned a fraction of an inch *forward*, her clear blue eyes brightening with the unguarded, fascinated curiosity of someone trying to puzzle out the clockwork beneath the casing.

And then she smiled at him, and told a house full of fire-wielders that she quite liked the rain.

A genuine, startled bark of laughter broke past Lucien's scarred mouth, the tension in his broad shoulders dropping an inch as a warm, boyish glint lit his russet eye.

"Not Vanserra heritage, no," Lucien said, a crooked grin tugging at the unscarred corner of his mouth as the tiny gold gears of his mechanical eye shifted with a soft *whir* to match her gaze. "Though if you say you prefer the rain too loudly under this roof, my lady, Eris is liable to evaporate the clouds over the valley out of sheer spite."

Beside the hearth, Jurian did not laugh.

Over the rim of his whiskey glass, the mortal general's dark eyes narrowed by a millimeter. He had watched the exact angle of her smile—that effortless, governess-drilled curve of the lips that took fifteen years of drawing-room tutors to perfect—and he had heard the crisp, unmistakable music of her vowels. *Forgive me the assumption.* Not Prythian border-peasant. Not merchant class.

And right beside her, where her hand rested in the crook of Eris's black-cherry sleeve, Eris's fingers tightened once over hers.

It was invisible to the two men across the flagstones, but against her skin, the temperature of Eris's palm spiked—a slow, warning brand of heat that made the golden bargain filigree along her forearm flare bright against the red-gold cuffs. She had followed the letter of his law—*speak when spoken to*—and used it to charm his brother, needle his bloodline, and hand Jurian a sample of Hightower elocution in a single breath.

When Eris looked down at her, his mouth wore a courtier's silk smile, but the molten amber of his eyes promised a reckoning the second the oak doors closed behind their guests.

"Lilly has developed an unfortunate fondness for damp evenings since crossing the border," Eris said smoothly, his voice a dark velvet purr as he drew her past the archway into the candlelit dining room. "Come to the table, both of you, before Briar's soup goes cold."

At the right side of the long oak table—directly beside Eris's high-backed carved chair at the head—Eris did not wait for Caius or Lucien to pull out her chair. He drew it back himself, his right hand brushing the bare skin at the nape of her neck for a fraction of a second as she sat, his thumb grazing the warm hollow just behind her collar where the low-cut velvet left her completely uncovered. Even as she settled into the seat, the high side-slit of the forest-green skirt parted over her left knee in the firelight; Eris smoothly flicked the heavy velvet fold back over her thigh with two fingers before taking his place at the head of the table, so close his knee brushed hers beneath the linen.

Across from her, Lucien took the left-hand chair, while Jurian claimed the seat further down, setting his empty whiskey glass beside the silver soup spoon as Caius glided in with a steaming tureen of roasted chestnut and wild mushroom bisque and a decanter of dark spiced Autumn red.

"Lilly," Jurian repeated, testing the two syllables on his tongue as he unfolded his linen napkin, his dark, cynical gaze resting squarely on her across the candlesticks. "A pretty name. Simple. We had a dozen girls named Lilly in the coastal towns south of the continent before the war—though none of them wore Vanserra faebane at the supper table, and none of them spoke with the cadence of the southern courts." He leaned an elbow on the oak, his human eyes locking onto hers with sudden, conversational sharpness. "Tell me, Lilly... which port did your ship sail from when you crossed the water?"

Before the last word had even left Jurian's mouth, beneath the heavy white tablecloth, Eris's boot pressed firmly against the side of Aurelie's ankle—a deliberate, scorching tap of warning—while above the table, Eris lifted his wineglass by the stem, his amber eyes fixed lazily on the mortal general.

"Eat your soup, Jurian," Eris murmured, though he didn't tell Aurelie *not* to answer; he tilted his head a fraction toward her, watching from two feet away to see how his creature would step around the snare.

## Turn 7 — Vesper (Eris, Lucien & Jurian)

Across the table, Lucien had just lifted his goblet of spiced Autumn red to his mouth.

At *"someone with so many years of adventures behind them has seen many ports,"* Lucien nearly choked on his wine.

He coughed once into his linen napkin, the bronze aperture of his mechanical eye spinning with an audible *whir-click* from Aurelie's serene, angelic face to Jurian's tightening jaw. Nobody—not in Velaris, not in Graysen's estate, not in the Band of Exiles—spoke to Jurian about his five centuries as Amarantha's trophy like a polite nineteen-year-old debutante inquiring after an old sea captain's voyages.

Jurian's silver spoon stopped dead an inch above his bowl.

For half a heartbeat, the roguish tavern-general vanished. The muscles along his stubbled jaw locked, and his dark human eyes went flat and lethal as river-ice. He heard the exact blade she had slid between his ribs—and worse, he heard *Lord Eris told me* wrapped around it like a silk ribbon. She knew what he had been for five hundred years, and she had just used it to shut his mouth on her port of origin while smiling at him like a girl at a vicarage tea.

Then Jurian slowly lowered his spoon into the chestnut bisque and let out a low, rasping chuckle through his nose, tipping his chin toward her in begrudging salute.

"Touché, little bird," Jurian murmured, his dark eyes glinting with a sudden, far more dangerous respect as he took a spoonful of soup. "No. Can't say I did much sailing for the last five hundred years. The view was... terribly repetitive."

At the head of the table, Eris did not laugh.

He had watched every fraction of the exchange from two feet away. First, he had seen the way her face softened in the foyer when Lucien laughed—the way her blue eyes lingered on the crinkles at the corner of his younger brother's russet eye, hungry for a warmth she refused to ask for under his roof. That alone had turned the amber in Eris's veins a shade hotter and uglier.

And then she had sat down, refused to look to him for permission, and gutted Jurian across his tablecloth with the exact knife Eris had handed her upstairs—before calmly lifting her spoon to her mouth as if butter wouldn't melt on her tongue.

Above the table, Eris took a slow, unhurried sip of his wine, his face a mask of bored aristocratic composure.

Beneath the heavy white linen tablecloth—where neither Lucien nor Jurian could see a thing—Eris moved.

He did not tap her boot in warning this time. Instead, his left hand slid silently across the narrow gap between their chairs, found the high side-slit where the heavy forest-green velvet parted over her left leg, and settled his bare, scorching palm directly onto the naked skin of her thigh.

His fingers didn't pinch and didn't hurry; they spread wide and possessive across the warm curve of her leg, his thumb stroking a single, slow inch upward against her bare skin right as she raised her second spoonful of soup. It was a wicked, silent reward for drawing blood at his table—and a ruthless territorial claim testing just how well a month of practice could keep her voice steady and her face blank while his hand burned against her thigh under the table.

"Lilly has a remarkably keen ear for history," Eris drawled smoothly over the rim of his glass, his thumb pressing a fraction deeper into the soft skin above her knee while his amber eyes stayed locked on Lucien across the candles. "And a fondness for asking how things are put together. In fact, brother, I believe she has been staring at your Dawn Court trinket since the second step of the stairs, trying to decide whether to ask if it runs on springs or spellwork."

Lucien blinked, glancing back at Aurelie, the defensive edge he usually wore around his scar melting away into wry amusement as he touched two fingers to the scarred ridge beside the whirring bronze sphere.

"Both, actually," Lucien told her warmly, leaning his forearms on the oak table as if grateful for an excuse to talk to her instead of his brother. "A tinkerer in the Dawn Court forged the housing out of spelled bronze and gold facets—it sees magic as well as flesh and bone, though it has an irritating habit of clicking whenever I'm trying to be quiet in the woods." His russet eye softened as it rested on her face, then flicked—just for a fraction of a second—toward the red-gold faebane cuffs clinking softly against her soup spoon. "Do they have tinkerers like that where you come from, Lilly? Or did my brother confiscate your books along with your freedom?"

## Turn 8 — Ruby (Aurelie & Prince)

Aurelie was eating her soup the entire time it took Jurian to react to her words. She glanced up at him, and smiled a little more genuine at his chuckle, but did not acknowledge his concession, as if an acknowledgment of her own jab would be far beneath her.   
On the second spoon, she felt the hand and almost inhaled the liquid into her windpipe. She froze, for a second, with her spoon in her mouth and her free hand on the tablecloth closed into a fist — though she stopped herself from clenching it.  
She closed her eyes, and inhaled through her nose, letting out a small, satisfied 'Mmm' and pouring the heat of his touch into the sound.   
When her eyes opened, she looked straight at Eris. She could feel the flush starting to form at her collarbones, and she put the spoon away and grabbed the wine glass with her other hand in one motion.  
"The kitchen really outdid itself today, my Lord." She said, then sipped wine. The alcohol was the only thing her mind could conceive that she could blame the blush on. "Were you hiding the best things from me, the entire time I've been here?"  
Her heart was racing. She wanted to reach her hand under the table and move his away. No one has ever touched her bare thigh. No man has ever touched her skin like that and she wasn't sure why her blood was starting to boil, or why a small treacherous part of her wanted his hand to move up, instead of away.  
But then Eris moved the conversation to Lucien and she looked at his brother and went an even deeper shade of red. Which actually worked in her favour.  
"I hope it wasn't impertinent of me to wonder. Mortal lands do have tinkerers, but none capable of such fine work, and certainly none that would be able to weave it with magic."  
She ate a mouthful of soup, to buy herself a little time.  
"And I didn't bring any books. I've barely had a day's worth of supplies when Lord Eris found me. I'm actually quite glad to have been organizing the library in Ashgrove for the past month. The books here are much more interesting than anything mortal lands have to offer."

## Turn 9 — Vesper (Eris, Lucien & Jurian)

That soft, closed-eyed *"Mmm"*—and the sheer, brazen nerve of looking straight into Eris's eyes with a rose-pink flush climbing her bare collarbones to ask if he had been hiding the best things from her—struck the head of the table like oil thrown on live coals.

For a fraction of a second, Eris's wineglass stopped halfway to his mouth. The molten gold in his eyes flared so bright the black of his pupils shrank to pinpricks.

Beneath the white linen tablecloth, his left hand answered her challenge.

Instead of retreating when she set down her spoon, his palm glided two slow, deliberate inches *higher* up her bare thigh, slipping past the parted edge of the forest-green velvet where the skin was untouched by the air of the room—soft, fever-warm, and entirely unguarded. His long fingers curved around the inside of her thigh, not quite at the apex, but high enough that his thumb traced a lazy, scorching circle against the delicate skin where her pulse fluttered under his touch.

"Hardly, Lilly," Eris murmured, his voice dark and smooth as warmed brandy as he met her gaze over the rim of his glass. "A palate has to be broken in slowly. Give someone the richest dish in the house on their first night, and they scarcely know what to do with it."

Across the table, Lucien missed the undercurrent entirely—he saw only the deep crimson blush blooming up her bare throat and cheeks as she apologized for staring at his eye, and the earnestness with which she spoke of the Ashgrove library.

Something almost tender crossed Lucien's scarred face. He reached for the cut-crystal pitcher at the center of the table and poured cool spring water into the empty glass beside her wine, a quiet, instinctive gesture to ease the heat of the spiced Autumn vintage he assumed was flushing her skin.

"Not impertinent at all," Lucien said gently. "Half the court stares and pretends they aren't looking; it's a relief when someone simply asks." A faint, nostalgic smile touched his mouth at the mention of the upstairs shelves. "And if you've survived a month in the Ashgrove library, you have my sympathy. Half those folios haven't been dusted since I lived under this roof. If you look behind the third shelf by the east window, there's still a stack of Dawn Court natural histories I hid from my tutors when I was fourteen."

Caius stepped in silently to clear the soup bowls, replacing them with platters of roast pheasant glazed in dark autumn berries and thyme, buttered roots, and a fresh bottle of deep red.

Down the table, Jurian picked up his carving knife, though he didn't touch his bird yet. He had been watching the two brothers and the girl between them with the patient, unblinking stillness of a hawk on a fencepost.

"A girl who crosses the sea on a brig with a single day's supplies," Jurian mused aloud, resting the flat of his knife against the rim of his plate, "and spends her captivity happily cataloguing High Fae histories in the eastern woods." He tilted his head, his dark human eyes flicking from Aurelie's flushed collarbones to Eris's impassive face. "Funny coincidence, really. Because the reason I rode north with Lucien from the coast this week wasn't to admire your oak trees, Vanserra."

Beneath the tablecloth, Eris's hand stilled on Aurelie's bare thigh—not pulling away, his palm remaining a heavy, burning weight against her skin, his thumb resting right over her pulse so he could feel the exact beat of her heart before she made a sound.

"Enlighten us, then, Jurian," Eris said lazily, cutting a slice of pheasant with his right hand while his left stayed buried in the slit of Aurelie's velvet skirt. "Before the bird gets cold."

"Queen Ismena's envoys landed three ships at the southern neutral ports ten days ago," Jurian said, his gravelly baritone dropping into the quiet between the crackle of the logs. Although he was answering Eris, his dark eyes drifted—almost casually—back to Aurelie's face to watch her swallow. "Officially, they're there to renegotiate timber and grain tariffs with the mortal territories. Unofficially? Their captains have been spilling gold in every harbor tavern from the coast to the Wall, looking for word of a highborn runaway from Hightower—and an item of royal property that vanished from Ismena's vaults the same week a certain unnamed brig cleared the narrows."

Lucien frowned, glancing sharply at Jurian and then at Eris. "Ismena? What does the southernmost mortal queen want on our side of the Wall?"

"That," Jurian said softly, lifting his wineglass while his eyes stayed locked on Aurelie's bare throat, where every breath and every swallow stood completely exposed in the candlelight, "is what I was hoping your brother—or his remarkably well-spoken librarian—might tell us."

Under the table, Eris's fingers gave a single, slow, commanding squeeze against the bare skin of her inner thigh—*steady, little wolf; don't give him an inch*—even as Eris leaned back in his carved chair and looked down the table at Jurian with cold, lethal amusement.

"And you rode all the way to Ashgrove to ask if I have taken up smuggling Queen Ismena's silverware, Jurian?" Eris asked silkily. He turned his head toward Aurelie, his molten amber gaze dropping for half a beat to her lips before rising to her eyes. "Go on, Lilly. Tell Lord Jurian whether you hid a chest of royal gold in your one day's supplies before you washed up in my woods."
