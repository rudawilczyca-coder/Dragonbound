# Chapter Two — The Forest House Return (Live Session Turns)
*Started: 30 October 2026*
*Setting: Ashgrove (Study → Dining Room), one week after the Map Dinner & Eris's departure for the Forest House.*

## Turn 1 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

The wards at the one-mile line did not ring when Eris crossed them; they *shivered*, like ahound's skin twitching at the touch of its master's spur.

For seven days, Ashgrove had breathed at a mortal's pace. The fires in the grates had burned like ordinary wood; the corridors had smelled of lemon oil, old paper, and the cold lake wind that drifted in whenever the girl came up from the water with her skirts damp at the hem.

At a quarter past six, before the stable lanterns were even half-lit, the iron gates at the bottom of the drive swung open and a lathered black gelding came up the gravel at a hard canter, six red-gold foxhounds running silent at his stirrups.

Eris swung down before Tomas had reached the mounting block.

He didn't look toward the lake, though his nostrils flared once—catching the unmistakable, ozone-bright tang of ancient magic pooled down by the water's edge, fuller and less ragged than it had been a week ago, and woven right through the house wards like a second spine.

His dark copper riding coat was stiff with road-salt from the northern passes and smelled faintly of woodsmoke—not Ashgrove's clean birch, but the heavy, resinous char of the Forest House, where Beron's hearth fires burned green with tallow and old spite. A thin, crescent-shaped split marked his lower lip, barely healed, though his posture as he stripped off his riding gloves one finger at a time was immaculate enough to slice glass.

"Walk him for twenty minutes before you give him grain," Eris told Tomas without breaking stride, slapping the reins into the stablemaster's palm. "He threw a shoe two miles past the border."

He took the front steps two at a time.

Caius was already waiting in the foyer with a silver tray and a fresh decanter, his grey eyes taking one look at the set of Eris's jaw before bowing a fraction deeper than usual.

"My lord. We did not expect you until the morning."

"My father's table lost what little charm it possessed around Tuesday," Eris said. His voice was quiet, clipped, and far too smooth. He unclasped his riding cloak and dropped it over Caius's outstretched arm, walking straight down the corridor toward his study. "Report."

Caius fell into step half a pace behind him, reciting the week's ledger with the steady precision of sixty years' service. "She has kept the perimeter, my lord. Six o'clock every morning in the south courtyard with Master Brennec, and a second hour in the afternoons at her own request. Until three in the library. From three until dusk down by the lake with the dragon, and occasionally the stables—Tomas tells me the auburn mare permitted her in the round pen on Thursday without taking a chunk out of her."

Eris stopped with his hand on the brass latch of his study door. Slowly, he turned his head.

"Brille?"

"Twenty minutes on the lunge line, my lord. No bones broken on either side."

Nothing in Eris's face softened; if anything, the amber in his eyes cooled by another degree. He pushed the study door open. The hearth inside was cold—only laid with fresh birch logs awaiting a spark. Eris didn't bother with a flick of his wrist; as he crossed the threshold, the logs caught with a sharp *whuff* of white-hot gold that made the shadows leap against the bookshelves.

He walked to his desk, where the carved wooden box sat at the corner of the blotter. With two fingers, he flipped the latch open.

Inside lay the two dull grey faebane bracelets, untouched since the morning he had ridden out and left her wrists bare to see what she would do with seven days of rope.

He lifted them out and set them side by side on the dark leather of the desk, where the firelight caught their dead, matte iron.

"She left five questions unanswered at my map table last week," Eris said, not looking back as he poured himself three fingers of dark spiced wine. "Tell the kitchen to hold dinner by half an hour. Then go down to the lake and inform our guest that she has five minutes to get out of the wind and into my study—or I will come down the hill and fetch her myself."

## Turn 2 — Ruby (Aurelie & Prince)

Seven days.

Seven days in which Aurelie threw herself into everything Ashgrove allowed her to touch, as if movement alone could scrape the memory of iron off her wrists.

Every morning at six she was in the south courtyard with Caelum, taking bruises on her forearms and learning how to fall without breaking herself—and when an hour wasn't enough to quiet her head, she asked him for a second one in the afternoons. Down at the stables, under Tomas's watchful eye, she spent twenty minutes in the round pen with Brille until the vicious auburn mare finally permitted her to sit without taking a piece out of her.

The hours until three belonged to the library. On the surface, she was still systematically cataloguing Eris's centuries of neglected shelves; underneath, every time she was alone between the stacks, she was hunting for any mention of *Feyre Archeron*, the Cauldron, or a mortal girl remade by Fae magic.

And then, from three until dusk—Prince.

With the faebane bracelets left behind in Eris's study, the bond between them wasn't muffled through thick glass anymore. It was wide open, warm and immediate in her chest. Every afternoon they walked the lawns in the golden hour, her bare palm resting against the white-and-ice-blue feathers of his flank as he grew taller and broader by the day, ending their evenings down by the quiet water of the lake.

They were sitting on the bank watching the last light fade across the water when Prince's head snapped up, his golden horns catching the dusk and his cat-like azure pupils narrowing to slits.

*He's back.*

Aurelie's stomach dropped, a cold knot tightening beneath her ribs.

She didn't get up right away. Even when she heard Caius's measured footsteps coming down the path to deliver the summons—five minutes, or the heir to Autumn would come down the hill himself—she deliberately stayed on the bank for two full minutes longer, refusing to run at the snap of Eris Vanserra's fingers.

Before she finally rose and brushed the dry grass from her skirts, she leaned in and pressed a kiss to the top of Prince's snout.

*We will be free of him one day.*

Caius was waiting for her at the top of the garden steps, his weathered face carefully neutral as he fell into step beside her through the darkening halls. Just before they reached the study door, the old steward spoke in an undertone barely louder than the rustle of her hem:

"He is not in a good mood, Miss. A week at the Forest House rarely improves his temper. You'd do well not to anger him today."

Aurelie set her shoulders, her hand already reaching for the brass handle. "I don't think he can get much worse than he already did."

She pushed the door open and stepped inside.

## Turn 3 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

"A bold hypothesis," Eris's voice came from the far side of the room, low and dry as scorched silk. "Let us see how it holds up by nine o'clock."

He had shed his road-stained riding coat, standing beside the heavy oak desk in a waistcoat of dark bronze brocade, his white linen cuffs turned back once over his forearms. One hand rested near the open wooden box on the blotter; the other held a cut-crystal glass of dark wine that he hadn't drunk from since her footsteps reached the hall.

The fire in the grate behind him was burning too high and too quiet—not snapping like honest birch, but clinging to the logs in sheets of heavy, unnatural amber that threw long shadows across the floorboards between them.

His eyes started at the hem of her skirts—where a crescent of damp lakeside moss still clung to the wool—traveled up past her waist, lingered for half a beat on the bare, unshackled skin of her wrists, and finally settled on her face.

He rested two knuckles on the edge of the desk, right beside the bracelets, without picking them up.

"Come here, Lilly."

He didn't point to the chair across from him. He waited until she had crossed the rug into the pool of amber firelight, close enough that she could smell the cold night wind, woodsmoke, and crushed pine needles still clinging to him beneath the sharper, darker scent of the Forest House.

"Let me see your hands," he said softly.

It wasn't a request, and his gaze didn't drop from her face as he waited.

"Brennec tells me you have been coming back to the courtyard for a second hour every afternoon," Eris continued, his tone deceptively conversational, like a tutor reviewing a ledger whose numbers had begun to irritate him. "Tomas tells me you spent twenty minutes in the round pen with a mare that took three fingers off the last stableboy who tried to longe her. And Caius tells me you have been very industrious in the third aisle of my library."

A slow, humorless smile touched one corner of his mouth, never reaching the molten gold of his eyes.

"You won't find *Feyre Archeron* in three-hundred-year-old histories of the Autumn Court, little mortal. My chroniclers are quick, certainly, but I generally prefer to wait until a High Lady has survived her first decade before I waste calfskin on her biography."

He tilted his head slightly, studying her face in the firelight—and the quiet, unmistakable steadiness that a week with her dragon in her head had put back into her spine.

"Seven days without the iron on your wrists," Eris murmured, his voice dropping half an octave, "and already you walk into my house as though I am the guest interrupting your routine. Tell me—before I decide whether those bracelets stay on the blotter tonight or go back onto your skin—what, precisely, did you think was going to happen when I rode back through those gates?"

## Turn 5 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

Eris caught the twitch of her mouth before it had finished forming.

His gaze flicked from her lips to the window behind her, where the dark outline of the pines stood against the last copper streak of dusk, and then back to her eyes.

"He thinks he's amusing," Eris said softly. "Good. I prefer a beast with enough spirit left to break."

He pushed off from the edge of the desk and stepped around it.

He didn't hurry, and he didn't stop at a polite distance. He walked straight into the space she was occupying until the toe of his riding boot brushed the hem of her skirt and she had to tip her head back further just to keep his face in view. Up close, the heat radiating off him wasn't the comfortable warmth of the study hearth; it was dry, fever-sharp, and smelled of scorched wool, claret, and crushed pine.

"I asked to see your hands, Lilly," he murmured. "Not to hear your audition for household prophet."

Before she could decide whether to raise them, his left hand caught her right wrist, and his right caught her left.

His fingers were bare, calloused at the base from sword-hilt and reins, and burning hot—hot enough that the air between their palms seemed to shimmer. He didn't reach for the faebane bracelets on the blotter. Instead, he turned her hands palms-up between them, his thumb pressing deliberately into the raw, reddened skin at the base of her thumb where Brille's lunge line and Caelum's wooden practice swords had rubbed her mortal flesh nearly to a blister.

He pressed just hard enough to make the nerve sing.

"How terribly domestic you make us sound," Eris said, his voice a low velvet purr right above her upturned face. "A predictable gaoler. A predictable dinner. A predictable bedtime with iron on your wrists while you lie in the dark and count the cracks in my ceiling."

Still holding both her wrists pinned in one of his large hands now, he reached back with his free hand and picked up a single faebane bracelet from the blotter. The dull grey metal was ice-cold as he rested its unlatched rim lightly against the bare, hammering pulse of her left wrist—not snapping it shut yet, just letting the dead, heavy chill of the iron kiss her skin right beside the searing heat of his thumb.

"Let me correct your ledger," he said quietly.

The fire in the grate dropped three inches at once, the study darkening until the amber glow in his eyes was the brightest thing in the room.

"First: I do not *regale* anyone with stories of my father's house. I spent seven days watching three of my brothers try to convince Beron that a strange white shadow spotted over the eastern ridge last month was a Winter Court incursion—and not something I am hiding in my woods." His thumb tightened a fraction over her pulse. "It cost me two border garrisons and a very tedious display of temper to point their hounds toward the frost-line instead of Ashgrove. And while I was paying for your sky, you were down in my library hunting for *Feyre Archeron* as though the Night Court would send anything for your dragon except a cage."

He leaned down an inch. The cold faebane cuff slid slowly up the inside of her forearm, tracing the path of the bargain mark beneath her sleeve without closing.

"Second: you will not be wearing these overnight."

His mouth curved—sharp, cruel, and wholly devoid of warmth.

"Putting them on you while you sleep is a waste of good iron. No, Lilly. Tonight you are going to sit across from me at dinner with your bond wide open, and you are going to answer three of the five questions you left on my table last week. Every time you lie to me, or stall, or give me a pretty half-truth, your dragon outside is going to hold a full-perimeter shield against my fire until his legs shake—and *you* are going to feel every second of it through that bond you missed so much."

He stopped the cold metal cuff in the crook of her elbow, his eyes locked on hers from six inches away.

"And if you manage not to bore me before the second course," Eris whispered, "perhaps I will let you keep the bracelets off until morning. Now—shall we test how much of the last month you actually learned, or would you like to revise your prediction?"

## Turn 7 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

The silence that followed her sneer lasted long enough for a log in the grate to crack in half, sending a spray of white-gold sparks up the chimney.

Eris did not step back.

Instead, as she leaned forward into his grip, he took the half-step she had offered and took two more beside it—crowding her straight backward until the backs of her thighs hit the heavy oak edge of his desk and there was nowhere left for her spine to go except up.

*Clack.*

He dropped the open faebane bracelet onto the leather blotter beside her hip without looking at it. He didn't let go of her wrists. His left hand kept both of them pinned between the linen of his shirt and the front of her dress, his thumb still pressed flat over the frantic, traitorous hammering of her pulse—counting every beat that gave the lie to her curl of lip.

Slowly, his free right hand came up. His bare fingers caught her beneath the jaw—not choking, not bruising, just a hard, burning cradle of thumb and forefinger along the bone of her chin that tilted her face another fraction higher and held it there.

"Look at you," Eris murmured.

His voice had lost every trace of courtly polish; it was rough as hearth-ash, pitched so low it vibrated against the skin of her wrists between their chests. His gaze dropped for half a second to her parted lips, then rose back to her eyes, the amber in his irises narrowing until the black pupils were thin as needles.

"Shaking like a trapped hare," he whispered, his thumb shifting a millimeter against the racing artery in her wrist so she knew *exactly* what he was reading on her skin, "and leaning into the teeth anyway."

A hot, ugly breath left him through his nose—half a laugh, half a snarl—brushing the loose curls at her temple. It infuriated him, and he did not bother to hide that it infuriated him: that seven days in his father's viper pit hadn't made his blood stir once, and thirty seconds in a room with this defiant, ink-smudged little liar had the fire under his collar rising until the air between their mouths tasted like copper.

"If my voice bores you, Lilly," Eris said softly, leaning down until his mouth was an inch from the shell of her ear, the heat of his cheek radiating against hers, "then by all means—let us listen to yours instead."

He didn't pull back when he spoke again; his jaw brushed the hair at her temple, his body caging her against the oak edge of the desk while outside, down by the darkening lake, the wards hummed faintly against the gathering presence of a very large, very displeased dragon.

"Tell me your first truth before we walk into that dining room," he breathed against her ear, his thumb pressing once—deliberately, slowly—over the brand-new sword blister on a palm that had never done a day's common labor before Ashgrove. "I let you play *Lilly* for my staff, and I leave your father and brothers breathing in Hightower, on the condition that you do not test the edges of our bargain."

His voice dropped to a whisper that only she could hear, the name leaving his tongue like a bare blade:

"So tell me, *Lady Aurelie*—when a girl who is forbidden to contact another court spends the seven days I am away tearing through my shelves for *Feyre Archeron* and the Night Court... were you merely satisfying a sudden scholarly curiosity, or were you looking for a way to send word over my borders behind my back?"

## Turn 9 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

Between their pinned wrists, the Ember Script along her left forearm and his right gave a single, slow pulse of heat—not the sharp sting of a violated clause, just the quiet, molten hum of magic recognizing an unvarnished truth.

She meant every word.

Eris felt that pulse travel up his arm into his chest, and the sound that came out of his throat wasn't civilized at all.

His gaze dropped—openly, unhurriedly, without a shred of the restraint she was fighting to keep—straight to her mouth. He watched her dry lips part on the threat. He smelled the sudden, dizzying shift in the air between them: rosewater and ink and cold lake-wind giving way to the unmistakable, traitorous heat of mortal blood racing just beneath flushed skin.

He didn't step back, and he didn't let her look away.

Instead, the thumb bracketing her jaw shifted upward—just half an inch—until the rough, calloused pad of it brushed straight across the center of her dry lower lip.

He pressed there, slow and deliberate, feeling the tremor of her breath hit his knuckle.

"Will I," Eris murmured.

His eyes lifted back to hers, molten gold swallowing the black of his pupils until there was almost nothing left between them but heat.

"How terribly gallant of you, Lady Aurelie. And here I thought you were tearing through my shelves to beg the Night Court for a rescue—when all along, my little mortal was looking for a recipe." His thumb traced a millimeter further toward the corner of her mouth before stopping. "*Feyre Archeron.* A human girl who walked over the Wall with nothing in her hands, and came back with enough teeth to gut a High Lord's house."

He leaned down until his lips were a hair's breadth from hers—close enough that she could feel the dry, scorching heat of his mouth with every word he spoke, close enough that if she leaned forward by the width of a fingernail, she would close the distance herself.

"Keep reading," he whispered against her mouth, a dark, vicious dare vibrating through his chest into her pinned hands. "Read every volume on those shelves. Let Brennec put a blade in your palm twice a day until your skin splits. Because if you ever manage to put a knife against my throat, Lilly, I promise you—I will be looking right at you when you try."

For two heartbeats, neither of them moved. His thumb stayed on her lower lip; her wrists stayed caged against his chest; the fire in the grate licked high and white-hot against the chimney stone.

Then—just as suddenly as he had crowded her—Eris let go.

He released her jaw and her wrists in the same breath, stepping back half a pace so the cool air of the study rushed abruptly into the inches where his body had been. Without taking his eyes off her flushed face, he reached down to the blotter, scooped up both open faebane bracelets in one hand, and dropped them into the pocket of his waistcoat.

The heavy iron gave a soft, unmistakable *clink* against his ribs.

"They stay in my pocket through the soup," Eris said, his voice smoothing back into silk even as the gold in his eyes burned bright and unbanked. He gestured toward the door leading out to the hall. "Walk ahead of me into the dining room, Lilly. Caius has been holding the roast for ten minutes—and you still owe me an answer on the Day Court."

## Turn 11 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)

In the study doorway, as she swept past him with that mocking little dip of her skirts, Eris didn't move an inch out of her path—forcing her sleeve to brush the front of his waistcoat as she went by.

Wait until her eyes closed. He had seen her eyelids flutter shut when his thumb was on her mouth—had felt the tiny, involuntary hitch in her breath right before she caught herself—and the knowledge sat in his chest like a live coal all the way down the corridor.

He walked three paces behind her down the stairs, unhurried, letting her hear the measured tread of his boots on the oak treads—and, with every step, the faint, heavy *clink* of the two open faebane bracelets shifting against each other inside his waistcoat pocket.

In the dining room, the parchment map of Prythian from the week before had been cleared away, replaced by white damask, heavy silver, and tall beeswax tapers that threw warm light across the polished walnut. One of his red-gold foxhounds lay stretched beside the hearth; as Aurelie took her customary chair at the right hand of the head of the table, the hound lifted its nose, scented the bare, unshackled magic humming through her skin from the lake, and gave a low, rumbling sigh before settling back onto its paws.

Eris took the carved high-backed chair at the head of the table, close enough that his left elbow rested a scant foot from her right wrist.

He said nothing while the two liveried servants ladled out the dark venison-and-rosemary broth and filled his crystal goblet with spiced claret. He merely leaned back against the carved oak, one long finger resting against the stem of his glass, his amber eyes watching the flush that still hadn't completely faded from the apples of her cheeks.

Only when the last servant bowed and pulled the heavy double doors shut behind him did Eris flick his thumb.

A thin, nearly invisible ribbon of gold fire ran around the seam of the doorframe and vanished into the wood, sealing the room against every ear in the house.

"Drop the theatrics?" Eris repeated softly.

He picked up his wineglass, swirling the dark red liquid once without drinking.

"My steward, my stablemaster, and my kitchen staff are loyal to me, little mortal—not to Lord Delacroix of Hightower. If a scullery maid or a supply carter hears the name *Aurelie* through a keyhole and sells it for three silver marks at a crossroads tavern, my brothers will not need to hunt your dragon across my borders. They will simply ride south and burn your father's estate to the cellar stones to see who comes flying out of the smoke."

He set the glass down with a quiet *tink* and leaned slightly toward her chair, his gaze dropping for half a heartbeat to her lower lip—right where the phantom heat of his thumb still lingered—before lifting back to her eyes.

"So in my corridors, while a single door remains unsealed, you are *Lilly*," Eris murmured, the velvet returning to his voice with an edge that could take skin off. "Behind a sealed door, at my table... if you want your own name in my mouth, *Aurelie*, you have only to keep earning it."

He picked up his silver spoon, gesturing lightly with it toward the space on the tablecloth where the map had lain seven nights ago.

"Now. Your memory seems to have suffered a sudden, tragic lapse somewhere between my study desk and the stairs, so allow me to refresh the ledger."

His left hand rested flat on the white linen between them—bare, ungloved, the faint tracery of the Ember Script visible just beneath his turned-back cuff.

"Last week, before I was obliged to go spend seven days reminding my father's house why they have not murdered me yet, you overturned four of my counters on this table. You gave me a passable textbook recitation on the Night Court, dismissed Spring for obvious reasons, and chose Summer because you fancied a pretty shoreline and an escape route by sea."

He dipped his spoon into his soup, his eyes never leaving hers.

"You left three things unexplained on my board. First: you tipped over the **Day Court** in the same breath as Night without a single word of justification—discarding Helion Spellcleaver and the ten thousand libraries of the continent's greatest scholar-king before the soup had even arrived. Second: you left **Dawn** and **Winter** standing untouched on the vellum as though they were furniture."

He took a slow sip of the broth, set the spoon down, and rested his chin lightly on his knuckles, watching her from a foot away.

"And third—right before Caius interrupted us with word from the Forest House—I told you that my estranged brother **Lucien** walks between the human lands and the Night Court, and that if I invite him to Ashgrove, Rhysand's spymaster will know what you are hiding in my woods before Lucien's horse has cleared the valley."

A slow, dangerous curve touched Eris's mouth. Outside, down by the dark water of the lake, a faint tremor of heat brushed against the perimeter wards—just enough to let her dragon know that the heir to Autumn was waiting.

"Eat your soup while it's hot, Aurelie," Eris said softly. "And start with the Day Court. Why did a girl who cannot keep her hands off my bookshelves throw out Helion Spellcleaver without looking twice?"

## Turn 13 — Vesper (Eris Vanserra)
