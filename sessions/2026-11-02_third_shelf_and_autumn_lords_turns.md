## Turn 1 — Vesper (Ashgrove Morning, Briar & The Third Shelf)

Morning came to Ashgrove clear, cold, and washed clean by the night's squall.

Pale gold autumn sunlight slanted through the tall balcony doors of Aurelie's bedchamber—still unlatched by the foot-wide gap Eris had flicked open from the terrace below—carrying the crisp scent of wet pine needles and woodsmoke into the room. On the floorboards near the foot of the bed, the forest-green velvet gown lay in a heap of bruised silk and painted gold leaves where she had torn it off in the dark.

At eight o'clock, a brisk double-knock sounded at the corridor door before Briar stepped inside balancing a steaming copper pitcher, a linen towel, and a breakfast tray of warm oat-cakes, honeyed figs, and black tea.

The Fae maid stopped dead two paces over the threshold.

Her amber eyes went first to the massive white-and-gold wyrm occupying the rug between the bed and the balcony doors, and then—as she set the tray hastily on the side table—straight to the hollow of Aurelie's bare throat.

In the clear morning light, the dried crimson fingerprint of High Fae blood sat stark and unmistakable over her pulse, darker than rust against mortal skin, with a faint matching trace at the corner of her jaw where Eris's thumb had dragged downward.

Briar's mouth parted by a fraction of an inch. Her gaze flicked from the dried blood on Aurelie's throat down to the crumpled green velvet dress on the floorboards, and back up again. Whatever conclusion the Autumn maid drew, she swallowed it behind pressed lips, her ears flattening slightly as she poured the steaming water into the porcelain washbasin and laid a clean, warm linen washcloth beside it—now that dawn had passed.

"Lord Vanserra rode out to the eastern ridge with Commander Caelum at first light," Briar said quietly, keeping her eyes respectfully on the washbasin rather than the mark at Aurelie's collarbones. "He left word that the morning training session is suspended while the south courtyard drains, and that the house is yours until the seamstress delivers the final alterations this afternoon."

With a quick curtsy, Briar slipped out and closed the door behind her.

***

An hour later, the second-floor library of Ashgrove was silent save for the slow tick of a brass astronomical clock on the mantel and the rustle of wind in the scarlet maples outside.

Three tall arched windows along the eastern wall poured shafts of morning sun across the worn Persian rugs and the towering floor-to-ceiling oak bookcases. Dust motes drifted lazily in the light. Unlike the formal dining room below, this room smelled of old vellum, beeswax polish, and cedarwood.

Counted from the east window, the **third bookcase** was wedged into the corner angle near a terrestrial globe.

Its eye-level shelf was packed tight with dry, uninviting folios bound in cracked brown calfskin: *Tonnage & Timber Tariffs of the Eastern Marches*, *Forest House Tithe Rolls*, and *Treatises on Border Ward-Stones*. None of the spines had been cracked in decades.

Yet when a hand reached past the row of heavy tax ledgers into the shadowy six-inch gap between the books and the cedar back-paneling, fingers brushed against something wrapped in soft, oiled sailcloth.

Pulled out into the shaft of eastern sunlight, the bundle held two slim volumes bound in supple Dawn Court sky-blue leather stamped with faded gold sunbursts—so different from Autumn's dark, severe bindings that they looked like stolen pieces of another world:

1. ***Mechanisms of Light & Lens: The Artificer's Compendium of Dawn*** — filled with intricate copperplate diagrams of prism-mirrors, clockwork joints, and refracting glass (the very craft that had fascinated a youngest brother centuries before he ever needed a mechanical eye).
2. ***A Natural History of the Southern Woods & Sky*** — a harmless illustrated treatise on migratory hawks, marsh-lights, and seasonal flora.

Except the second book didn't close flat.

Wedged deep into the hollow of its spine—folded into tight squares and hidden between two glued-together endpapers where no casual inspection would ever find them—were four sheets of heavy, weather-proofed vellum.

Unfolded in the sunlight, they were not Dawn Court prints at all. They were **hand-drawn escape maps of the Autumn Court's southern marches**, sketched in Lucien's own slanted hand from the years before he fled his father's territory:
* The first sheet mapped the **Ashgrove estate perimeter** in meticulous detail—marking the exact blind spots between the ancient ward-stones along the eastern creek bed, the timing of the border patrols' horn-calls, and a hidden culvert beneath the old root-cellar wall where a traveler on foot could slip past the outer gate without triggering the main road's fire-wards.
* The second and third sheets traced an overgrown **bridle-path and smugglers' trail** winding south through the deep redwoods, bypassing every Vanserra watchtower and garrison post between Ashgrove and the **Spring Court border**.
* And on the final sheet—drawn around an abandoned stone way-shrine half a mile inside the Spring border, just north of the river crossing toward the mortal lands—Lucien had marked an **X** beside the old milestone, accompanied by a note written in faded boyhood ink: * Safe shelter under the roots. Wait for the third bell after dawn.*

That was why Lucien had reminded her of the third shelf on his way out the door.

Having heard her speak at full volume across the dinner table while Eris stood fifteen feet away, Lucien had convinced himself she had only rejected his offer aloud to fool her captor's High Fae ears—and that once she found his boyhood escape route to the Spring border, she would slip through the creek-bed blind spot and meet him at the way-shrine.

Yet right on the inside cover of the sky-blue Dawn Court book itself—just beneath a fourteen-year-old Lucien's cramped warning (*Property of L. Vanserra. Touch this and lose a finger*)—sat a second line of ink, centuries old, written in the razor-neat, slashing copperplate hand that matched the shipping ledgers downstairs:

> *If you are going to hide contraband behind my tax ledgers, little brother, at least wrap them in oilcloth so the autumn damp doesn't warp the bindings. — E.*

The oiled sailcloth around the bundle was not Lucien's.

## Turn 3 — Vesper (Brille, Tomas & Eris at the Round Pen)

In the damp, sweet-smelling sand of the round pen, Brille tossed her dark head once, snorting a plume of white steam into the crisp autumn air before settling into a long, springy trot along the timber rail.

Two weeks ago, the blood-bay mare had pinned her ears flat and tried to take a stable-lad's fingers off for reaching toward her bridle. Now, as she circled the pen unclipped, her inside ear stayed swiveled steadily toward Aurelie in the center—waiting for the slightest shift of her shoulders or the drop of her hand, her glossy coat gleaming copper-red in the late morning sun.

Over by the open barn doors, Tomas paused with a pitchfork of fresh clover hay balanced across his calloused palms. A slow, weathered grin creased the old stablemaster's cheek.

"Drop your left shoulder an inch when you want her to turn in, Aura," Tomas called out warmly across the yard before hoisting the hay into the wheelbarrow. "She's watching your chest now, not the fence. Damned if you didn't take the vinegar right out of her."

Before the mare could complete her turn, a sudden hush fell over the stable-yard.

Down the gravel lane from the eastern ridge came the deep, rhythmic thunder of two heavy warhorses returning from the border line.

Brille's head snapped up, her nostrils flaring wide as Eris Vanserra's massive black stallion rounded the corner of the stone paddock wall, flecked with white lather at the chest, with Commander Caelum's dapple-gray gelding half a length behind.

At the sight of the High Fae lord, Tomas immediately straightened beside his wheelbarrow, touching two fingers to his cap and ducking inside the tack room with Caelum close behind him—neither man eager to linger in the open yard.

Eris swung down from his saddle in a single fluid motion, tossing the leather reins to a waiting groom without looking at him. He wore a dark riding coat of boiled russet leather stained at the hem with red forest mud, his copper hair tied back at the nape with a black cord, his riding gloves tucked into his belt.

He walked straight to the timber rail of the round pen and stopped, resting both bare forearms along the top plank.

For three quiet seconds, his molten amber eyes followed Brille as the high-strung mare slowed from a trot to a collected walk and curved inward toward Aurelie's side instead of bolting for the gate—putting herself almost protectively between the girl in the center of the ring and the lord at the fence.

Then Eris's gaze shifted from the mare to Aurelie.

His eyes traveled unhurriedly from the flush the cold air had put in her cheeks, down to the base of her throat—scrubbed clean of last night's blood now, though the pale skin at the hollow of her collarbones was still faintly pink from the washcloth—and then stopped dead on her wrists.

Where the sleeves of her riding tunic met her hands, her skin was completely bare.

No red-gold faebane cuffs. Only the delicate, gleaming gold filigree of his bargain mark winding around her left forearm in the sunlight, pulsing faintly with the wide-open channel between her mind and the hunting wyrm in the woods half a mile away.

On the top rail of the fence, Eris's bare right hand flexed once—and right on the pad of his thumb, a sharp, dark-red crescent where her teeth had sunk to the bone the night before stood out raw against his skin.

Instead of staying behind the timber barrier, Eris unlatched the heavy wooden gate of the round pen, stepped inside onto the churned sand, and clicked the iron bolt shut behind his back.

Brille snorted sharply, shifting her weight, until a single cold look from Eris made the mare sidestep three paces away and stand quivering by the far rail.

"The mare didn't let three of my best grooms near her without a twitch last month," Eris said conversationally, walking across the sand toward the center of the ring until he was four paces from Aurelie. His voice was low enough that neither Tomas nor Caelum inside the barn could hear a word. "Apparently she prefers creatures who bite."

He stopped directly in front of her, tall and mud-splattered and radiating the dry, woodsmoke heat of the border ride, his golden eyes dropping pointedly to her bare wrists and then lifting back to her face.

"Remind me, Lilly," Eris murmured, tilting his head a fraction of an inch. "When I left the bracelets beside your wineglass, did I tell you that you could leave them off inside your bedchamber—or did I tell you to have them back on your skin before you stepped outside my doors?"

## Turn 5 — Vesper (Eris at the Round Pen)

Eris watched the blood-bay mare pivot in a tight, loyal U-turn to shadow Aurelie's hip—putting nine hundred pounds of high-strung horseflesh between him and the girl circling toward the far side of the pen.

He did not miss why Aurelie was keeping her voice measured and her footsteps slow in the sand. She wasn't retreating for her own sake; she was shielding the mare from whatever temper she expected him to unleash.

A faint, humorless curve touched the corner of Eris's mouth.

Without taking his golden eyes off Aurelie's over-the-shoulder smirk, he gave a single low, two-note whistle and flicked two fingers of his left hand toward the side-gate at the back of the pen, which opened onto the sunny clover pasture.

With a crisp *clack* of amber magic, the iron paddock latch slid back and the wooden gate swung wide.

Brille's ears pricked forward at the sight of the open grass. The mare hesitated for half a second, gave Aurelie's shoulder an affectionate bump with her velvety nose, and then trotted briskly out into the sunny pasture—Before the gate swung shut and bolted itself behind her tail.

Leaving Aurelie completely alone with him inside the timber ring.

"Respect is for equals who keep their word, Lilly—not for little dragons who test how far down the driveway they can walk bare-wristed before I notice," Eris said smoothly, cutting straight across the center of the churned sand in four long, unhurried strides that intercepted her half-circle right by the wooden rail.

Before she could slip past his shoulder, he lifted his left hand into the open air and snapped his fingers once toward the second-story balcony of the manor house.

High above the stable-yard, a faint metallic chime rang out. Two seconds later, twin streaks of dull red-gold flashed through the late-morning sunlight and slapped cleanly into Eris's open palm—summoned straight from her bedchamber by the blood-ward forged into their metal.

He stepped into her space until the toe of his muddy riding boot brushed hers, leaving her with the sun-warmed timber rail of the round pen directly at the small of her back.

"Hold out your left arm," he said quietly.

He did not wait to see if she would obey. His left hand closed warm and iron-firm around her bare wrist, right over the gleaming gold filigree of his bargain mark, lifting her arm between them. With a single practiced motion, he snapped the first heavy faebane cuff shut around her skin.

The metallic *click* hit like a dropped portcullis. Instantly, the wide-open sensory window into the sunlit woods—the rustle of bushes, the scent of pine and prey, the warm hum of Prince's presence—was choked down to a distant, muffled throb behind a wall of cold lead.

Yet Eris did not immediately fasten the second cuff onto her right wrist.

Instead, he let the open bracelet dangle from his left fingers while he raised his bare right hand—the hand that had held his stallion's reins all morning—and rested the side of his index finger beneath her chin, tilting her face up into the autumn sun.

Right on the pad of his thumb, inches from her mouth, the deep, dark-red crescent of her bite mark stood out raw and unhealed against his skin.

"As a matter of fact, it throbbed every time I checked the stallion on the eastern ridge," Eris murmured, his voice dropping to a low, silk-rough register as he braced his other forearm on the top rail beside her hip, caging her against the wood. Slowly, deliberately, he brushed the scarred pad of his thumb across the corner of her smirking lower lip—the exact spot she had licked clean of his blood the night before. "Which turned out to be quite useful. Every time the cut pulled against the leather, it reminded me that my houseguest still has far too much spirit left in her teeth."

His thumb slid from the corner of her mouth down to her jawline, his golden eyes dark and steady on hers, the dry heat of his skin cutting straight through the crisp morning chill.

"You told me you were lonely without me here to push you around," he whispered, catching her right wrist in his hand and locking the second faebane cuff around her pulse with a slow, deliberate *snap* before keeping her wrist pinned lightly against the timber rail. "How fortunate for you that my morning patrol is finished. Since you forget my rules when you are left unsupervised in the stables, your afternoon freedom is revoked. The seamstress is waiting upstairs with the crimson wool for tomorrow night's supper—and you are going to try it on for me right now so I can see how well you hold a decanter without spilling a drop."

## Turn 7 — Vesper (Eris — The Walk & The Crimson Wool Fitting)

The wooden gate of the round pen clicked shut two inches in front of Eris's chest.

He didn't bother touching the iron latch.

Resting one bare palm lightly on the top plank, Eris vaulted the four-foot timber rail in a single effortless bound, his boots landing with a soft crunch in the fallen maple leaves five paces behind her.

He did not hurry to overtake her. Instead, for the entire sixty-yard walk from the stables to the side terrace doors, he strolled directly at her back—close enough that she could feel the weight of his golden eyes tracking the bounce of the chestnut knot at her nape and the snug, shameless fit of the riding trousers with every stride she took up the gravel path.

Only when she reached the stone steps of the side entrance did his hand reach past her hip to push the heavy oak door open for her, his chest brushing a hair's breadth behind her shoulder as she stepped across the threshold.

"An island in the mortal lands sounds terribly dull, Lilly," Eris murmured into her ear from behind, his voice low and dryly amused. "Besides, if you didn't want me admiring the tailoring on those breeches for the entire length of the driveway, you shouldn't have shut the gate in my face and insisted on walking ahead of me."

Ten minutes later, behind the carved cedar folding screen in her upstairs bedchamber, Briar helped Aurelie out of the riding tunic and trousers and slipped the altered gown over her shoulders.

It was not the low-cut emerald velvet of Lucien's dinner, nor was it the shapeless russet sack of a kitchen maid.

It was the color of fresh arterial blood spilled on snow—a fine, heavy-draped merino wool dyed deep crimson, tailored by the Autumn seamstress to fit Aurelie's torso from shoulder to hip like a gauntlet. The sleeves were long and narrow, ending just above the dull red-gold bands of the faebane cuffs so every guest at the table would see the shackles at her wrists. The collar rose high and severe around her neck like a court uniform—except at the front, where it parted in a narrow, diamond-shaped keyhole right over the hollow of her throat, framing the exact pulse-point where Eris's blood had dried that morning.

When Aurelie stepped out from behind the screen, the back of the bodice was still half-unlaced, the dark-gold cords hanging loose down her spine.

Across the room, Eris had shed his mud-stained riding coat and stood near the foot of her four-poster bed in a black linen shirt open at the throat, his copper hair loose over his shoulders.

He was standing directly beside her **bedside table**.

Right beneath his resting left hand lay the old leather-bound volume on *Summer Court Customs and Culture*—its pages slightly swollen in the center where Lucien's four folded vellum escape maps were wedged inside.

For one heart-stopping second, Eris's long fingers drummed once against the upper cover of the book, his eyes dropping to the embossed title on the spine.

"Studying up on Adriata already?" Eris asked mildly, his knuckles resting half an inch above the hidden border maps. "Ambitious of you, considering we haven't survived my father's bannermen yet."

Instead of opening the volume, he lifted his hand from the leather cover, picked up a heavy, cut-crystal wine decanter filled with water from the tray beside it, and turned toward her.

His gaze swept over her in the crimson wool—from the high, severe collar framing the pale hollow of her throat, down the tight line of the scarlet waist, to the faebane gleaming at her wrists. Something dark and appreciatively cold flickered in the amber of his eyes.

"Leave us, Briar," Eris said without looking at the maid. "Take the pins to the seamstress downstairs."

Briar dipped a silent curtsy, gathered the discarded riding clothes from the screen, and slipped out the door, clicking it shut behind her.

Eris set the crystal decanter on the small dressing table in front of the tall gilded pier-glass mirror and motioned with two fingers for Aurelie to stand before the glass.

When she stepped in front of the mirror, he walked up directly behind her back.

In the reflection, he towered half a head taller than she was, the black linen of his shirt framing the blood-red wool of her shoulders. Slowly, without asking permission, his warm, bare hands took hold of the loose dark-gold cords at the middle of her back.

With a steady, deliberate pull, he drew the first cross-lace tight—bringing the crimson wool snug against her ribs and pulling her half an inch backward toward his chest.

His knuckles brushed the thin linen of her shift along her spine as he worked the next cord upward, his scarred right thumb grazing the bare skin at the nape of her neck just below her pinned-up hair.

"Tomorrow night," Eris said softly, his golden eyes meeting hers in the mirror while his hands tightened the final lace between her shoulder blades, "Lord Vastor, Lady Meridia, Lord Cassander, and Lord Bellon will sit at my table. Vastor commands three hundred lances on the eastern road. Meridia sells whispers to my brother Kallum whenever she thinks my back is turned. And Cassander..."

Eris tied off the dark-gold knot at the base of her neck, his fingers lingering against the warm skin of her nape before sliding around her shoulders to pick up the heavy crystal decanter from the dressing table and press it into her hands.

"...Cassander believes that any mortal in an Autumn house is there to be broken for the table's amusement," Eris finished quietly, keeping his hands over hers on the cut crystal for a beat so she felt the full weight of the vessel. "They are coming here to see if the rumor of a girl at Ashgrove means I have gone soft. You will stand behind my right shoulder. You will keep your eyes down, your mouth shut, and this decanter steady. Do you understand me, Lilly—or are you planning to test how well you swim in the southern moat?"

## Turn 9 — Vesper (Eris in the Mirror)

When Aurelie didn't step forward toward the glass—when instead she deliberately sank her weight backward, pressing the laced curve of her spine and the swell of her hips flush against the front of his body—Eris went dead still behind her.

In the gilded mirror, the lazy courtier's mockery vanished from his face.

His chest expanded once against her shoulder blades in a slow, silent breath. Through the thin black linen of his shirt and the tight crimson merino of her bodice, the temperature of his skin spiked ten degrees in a single heartbeat—a dry, radiating furnace heat that soaked straight through the wool into her back.

Instead of stepping back to re-establish distance, Eris leaned into the contact.

His left hand slid off the cut-crystal decanter and wrapped slowly, possessively around the front of her waist, his long fingers splaying across the blood-red wool over her lower ribs to lock her back hard against his chest and hips—making sure she felt every unyielding inch of what she had just chosen to lean against.

At the same time, his right hand slid from the dark-gold knot at her nape around the side of her neck.

His palm curved warm and heavy against the column of her throat just above the high crimson collar, while the scarred pad of his thumb—marked with the dark-red crescent of her teeth—dropped straight into the narrow diamond keyhole at the hollow of her collarbones, pressing lightly over her jumping pulse.

"If Cassander or Vastor so much as brushes the hem of your sleeve tomorrow night, Lilly," Eris said softly, his molten gold eyes locked dead on hers in the mirror, "he will ride back to the Forest House with one hand in his saddlebag."

"Do not flatter yourself that it would be out of chivalry," he murmured, his breath hot against the side of her neck while his thumb stroked once across the bare skin inside the keyhole cutout. "Everything inside this crimson wool is on my ledger until I say otherwise. And I do not share my table—or my bites—with my father's dogs."

His left arm tightened around her waist by a fraction of an inch, holding her flush against the hard line of his body in front of the glass so she couldn't pretend she hadn't started it.

"Now be very careful how hard you lean back against the stove just to prove you aren't afraid of the fire, little dragon," Eris whispered against her ear, his voice dropping to a low, dangerous purr that vibrated straight into her spine. "Keep pressing yourself against me like that in your own bedchamber, and I might decide your endurance training for tomorrow night needs to start twenty-four hours early."

## Turn 11 — Vesper (Eris's Answer & The Autumn Lords' Dinner Begins)

*Hot for a human girl.*

For half a second—before his mask locked back into place—genuine, disbelief-laced irritation flashed through Eris's chest like a struck flint.

Five hundred years of High Fae lords, blood-sworn commanders, and court vipers dropping their eyes when his temper heated the air—and this nineteen-year-old mortal girl, shackled in his own faebane and laced into wool he had paid for, had the sheer, maddening nerve to lean her spine into his chest, bare her throat in front of his teeth, and mock him to his face.

Worse—what pissed him off more than her mouth—was that she wasn't wrong.

He hadn't meant for his skin to spike ten degrees the second her hips pressed back against him. He had come upstairs intending to inspect the seamstress's work, remind her of her place behind his chair, and return to his study. Instead, one deliberate press of her back through crimson wool had sent his blood roaring into his ears, thick and hot and completely unbidden—and now she was tilting her head to offer him the pale column of her neck as if *she* were the one holding the leash.

*Unbelievable,* he thought darkly, his jaw tightening by a fraction of an inch as a sharp, unwanted jolt of heat coiled low in his gut at the sight of her bared throat in the glass. *She weighs nothing, she has been in Prythian five weeks, and she doesn't have the common sense to be terrified when she corners a predator.*

And yet the sheer audacity of it—the way she refused to cower, the way she looked straight into his eyes in the mirror and dared him to bite—hooked into his ribs with a fierce, dark fascination he couldn't shake even when it infuriated him.

Refusing to let her see that she had caught him flat-footed, Eris narrowed his golden eyes to slits in the gilded glass and called her bluff.

He dipped his head into the curve of her bared shoulder until his mouth brushed directly against the damp, fever-warm skin just below the hinge of her jaw—not a kiss, but a slow, open-mouthed graze of heat and the razor-sharp edge of a High Fae canine right over the artery she had just exposed to him.

Beneath the scarred pad of his thumb inside the crimson keyhole at her collarbones, her pulse was hammering like a trapped finch against his skin. Good. Let her feel that she wasn't as immune as her smirk pretended.

"Hot for a human girl?" Eris murmured against her bare throat, his voice rougher than the smooth courtier's drawl he had aimed for as his left hand slid up from her waist to close over her hands on the cut-crystal decanter.
