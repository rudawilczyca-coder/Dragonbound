I. Overview
Name: Ashgrove
Type: Private estate — part hunting lodge, part manor, part fortress. Not an official Autumn Court holding. It exists on no court registry. Beron knows of it; he does not know what happens inside it.
Location: Deep Autumn Court territory, built against a forested hillside. Remote. A single road leads in through dense woodland; you would not find Ashgrove by accident.
Owner: Eris Vanserra. He did not build it.
Current Residents: Eris (intermittent), Aurelie Delacroix, Prince (Princeth Dia Yang), Caelum Brennec (contracted, temporary), household staff.
II. History
Ashgrove is old. Older than Eris, older than the Vanserra line as it currently exists, possibly older than the modern configuration of the Autumn Court itself. The bones of the structure — the foundation stones, the raw rock wall that forms the library's eastern face, and the wards — predate anything Eris can reliably date. He found references to the site in documents so old the ink had turned to rust, using a name in a dialect no one in the current court speaks.
The lodge carries the feeling of a place that was waiting to be found. It has its own memory. Rooms that Eris has never fully catalogued. A library wall that sweats mineral water in winter. A foundation that hums, faintly, on the solstices. He does not discuss these details with anyone.
The fox-and-flame crest: In the unmarked second room behind the main library, several hand-bound volumes bear a crest Aurelie does not recognize from the modern Vanserra heraldry — a fox wreathed in flame rather than the antler motif Beron's line uses. This is Eris's personal sigil, older than Beron's imposed heraldry, used only in private. Aurelie has seen the crest. Eris does not yet know she has seen it.
III. Grounds & Approach
The Approach
A single road through dense forest, winding uphill. The canopy thickens as you approach until the road narrows to barely two horses abreast. The lodge appears through the trees without warning — one moment you're in deep woods, the next the treeline breaks and Ashgrove is there, stone and timber against the hillside, as though it grew from the rock rather than being placed on it.
The Courtyard
Gravel. Roughly oval. This is the front of the lodge — the entrance Caius waits at, the space where horses are dismounted, where arrivals are received. Large enough for a small party of riders but not for a carriage. The gravel is old and well-packed; it crunches distinctively underfoot. Eris's foxhounds know the sound of every regular footstep on this gravel.

The Lake
A small, spring-fed lake at the base of the slope, roughly ten minutes' walk from the lodge. Not large — you could swim across it in five minutes — but deep, cold, and clear. Ringed by birch and alder on three sides, open to the cleared slope on the lodge side. The water is still most mornings; mist rises from it at dawn.
The lake is where Caelum runs past at first light. It's where Aurelie will eventually go when she needs to breathe. It's where the wards shimmer most visibly at dawn and dusk, reflected in the water like a second sky.
The Stables
A separate stone building across the courtyard from the lodge's main entrance — its own structure, its own world. A short walk in the rain, far enough that what happens there has genuine privacy from the lodge's windows. Houses 6-8 horses (Autumn-bred, larger and sharper than mortal stock), a tack room, a hay store, and a small quarters space for the stablehand.
Key occupants:
Brille — the auburn mare with the white star. Difficult. Hasn't been ridden in two years. Aurelie's secret project.
Tomas — stablehand, lesser fae, 60s, gruff, has a daughter. Reports to Caius technically; reports to no one in practice. He has chosen Aurelie quietly and without announcement.
Eris's black gelding and the grey mare Aurelie rode from Spring territory are stabled here.
The Forest
Autumn Court woodland — old growth, dense canopy, the trees turning with seasonal magic that is visible at Ashgrove. By mid-September the colors are already deepening in ways that aren't entirely natural. Copper, amber, rust, occasional flashes of deep crimson. The forest is alive in the fae sense — aware, responsive, not hostile but watching. The road out is the only reliable path. Going off-trail is possible but inadvisable; the woods close behind you.
IV. The Wards
Nature
The wards predate Eris. They predate the Vanserra line. Whoever laid them understood containment in a way that modern ward-crafters do not replicate. Eris left them entirely untouched when he claimed Ashgrove because they were already better than anything he could commission or modify.
Function
The wards dampen magical signatures outward without suppressing the magic inside. Think of a soundproofed room: Prince can blaze like a magical bonfire inside the perimeter and the world beyond doesn't hear it. The moment he crosses the perimeter — different story. His unshielded signature would broadcast to every magic-sensitive being in range.
This is why Prince does not need to shield while inside Ashgrove. His shielding training is preparation for eventually leaving the wards — for the court tour, for his expanded freedom under the bargain terms. Inside the perimeter, he is safe to be himself at full volume.
Perimeter
Approximately one mile radius from the lodge in all directions. This is the boundary Eris codified into the bargain's first clause (Prince may leave the lodge but must remain within a one-mile perimeter). He did not explain to Aurelie that the one-mile limit maps exactly to the ward boundary. Explaining would have given her information she could use. Eris does not give people complete information.
The perimeter encompasses: the lodge, the courtyard, the stables, the cleared ground, the south courtyard training area, the lake, and a substantial stretch of surrounding forest. It is large enough to feel like freedom. It is not freedom.
Visibility
The wards shimmer at dawn and dusk — visible as heat-haze, a faint distortion in the air that catches the light like oil on water. At the lake, the shimmer reflects on the surface and doubles itself. The rest of the day and night, they are invisible.
Aurelie cannot feel them (she is not yet magic-sensitive enough). Prince feels them constantly — a warm, low hum at the edge of his awareness, like standing inside a bell that was struck hours ago and is still faintly vibrating. He finds them respectful rather than threatening. They are old magic. He is old magic. They recognize each other.
Eris feels them as a familiar pressure, like the walls of a room he knows well. He can sense when something crosses the perimeter — inbound or outbound — as a change in that pressure. This is how he knew the Summer Court patrol was approaching during the road north, before anyone else did.
V. Interior — Ground Floor
The Entrance Hall
Stone floor, dark timber walls, a single long table where outdoor things accumulate — gloves, cloaks, the foxhounds' leads. A staircase climbs from here to the second floor. The hall is not grand; it is functional. It says this is a place where people live and work, not this is a place designed to impress you. Antlers mounted above the main door — old, from actual hunts, not decorative.
Eris's Study
Ground floor, accessible from the entrance hall. This is where he works — correspondence, strategy, the machinery of the coup. Heavy desk, a hearth he tends himself, shelves of current documents. The window overlooks the courtyard and, beyond it, the stables. He can see who comes and goes without leaving his chair.
After the dress corridor scene in Chapter 1: He locked himself in here, burned through two logs of his own hearth-fire, and reassembled himself before dinner. The study is his decompression chamber. When he's in here with the door closed, even Caius knocks.
The Dining Room
Ground floor, adjacent to the kitchen. Long enough for a table that seats eight comfortably, twelve at a push. The table is old oak, scarred from use. Candles rather than fae-lights — Eris prefers real flame in his own space. Windows on the south side overlook the cleared ground and, distantly, the lake.
The dinner scenes all happen here: the bargain dinner, the bracelet scene, the ongoing combative/silent/skipped meals of the early chapters.
The Kitchen
Ground floor, connected to the dining room by a service corridor. Run by the household cook (unnamed as yet). Functional, well-stocked, warm. The servants eat here. The service entrance opens to the side of the lodge, away from the main courtyard — useful for anyone who wants to come and go without being seen from the study window.
Servants' Quarters
Ground floor, rear of the lodge, tucked into the hillside. Small, clean, practical rooms. Caius has the largest. Briar shares with another lesser fae girl. Tomas sleeps in the stable quarters more often than in the lodge. The servants' area is accessible from the kitchen corridor — physically close to the working spaces but separated from the residential wings above.
VI. Interior — Second Floor
The East Wing Hallway
The emotional spine of the lodge. A long corridor running the full length of the east wing, second floor. Aurelie's green room is at one end. Eris's chambers are at the other. Between them: four or five closed doors (unused guest rooms, a linen store, a small sitting room no one uses). The hallway is lit by fae-lights that dim automatically after midnight but never fully extinguish.
Every footstep carries. The floorboards are old and they talk. She knows when he paces at 3am. He knows when Prince makes sounds during her nightmares. The proximity is inescapable and neither of them has acknowledged it aloud.
The corridor scene from Chapter 1 — where Aurelie collided with Eris wearing Mor's dress — happened in this hallway, roughly midway between their doors.
Aurelie's Room — The Green Room
East wing, second floor, south-facing. Named for the deep green wallcovering (faded, old, probably original to the lodge). Furnished simply: a bed large enough for her and a dragon that is rapidly outgrowing it, a wardrobe, a writing desk, a washstand. The trunk containing Mor's yellow dress and other old clothing sits against the far wall — Aurelie has not opened it again since the corridor scene.
The balcony: South-facing, overlooking the cleared ground and the lake beyond. Prince eats here at dawn. It is wide enough for him at his current size but he is growing. By mid-Chapter 3, he barely fits. By Chapter 4, he will need to eat on the ground below.
Eris's Chambers
East wing, second floor, opposite end from the green room. Larger than Aurelie's — a bedroom and an attached sitting room that functions as a secondary workspace. Sparsely furnished for a High Lord's heir: the bed is good but not ornate, the linens are dark, there are no personal items on display. The room of a man who has trained himself not to leave evidence of who he is in the spaces he occupies.
His window overlooks the same south-facing view as Aurelie's balcony — the cleared ground, the lake, the wards shimmering at dawn. They are looking at the same landscape from opposite ends of the same hallway. Neither has remarked on this.
The Library
Second floor, western wall — the full description from Chapter 1 stands. A long, deep, vaulted room hewn into the hillside itself. The eastern wall is raw mountain rock, dark, damp-cool, veined with mineral threads that catch firelight like copper wire. Two storeys of shelves on three walls, ladder-rails running the full circuit. Dark wood, centuries of smoke and hands.
Eris's note on the door: "The east shelves are mine. Touch what you like. The locked cabinet is locked for reasons. — E."
The locked cabinet contains documents related to the coup — correspondence, intelligence reports, plans. Aurelie has not attempted to open it. She has noted its existence.
The unmarked second room: Behind an unmarked door at the back of the main library — not locked, not hidden, simply unannounced. Older volumes. Hand-bound. Some in languages Aurelie cannot read. This is where the fox-and-flame crest appears on several spines. The room is smaller, more private, and feels older than the main library. It is the room where Eris kept the books that mattered to him before the coup consumed everything.
VII. Interior — Third Floor
The smallest level — the lodge narrows as it climbs the hillside. Fewer rooms, lower ceilings, the timber more exposed. This floor contains:
Storage rooms — old furniture, seasonal items, things that have accumulated over centuries and been forgotten.
Caelum's quarters (when in residence) — a spare, clean room at the top of the stairs. He chose it himself; it was the room furthest from both Eris and Aurelie. He does not personalize it. His knives sit on the windowsill. His bow leans against the wall. That is the full extent of his mark on the space.
Two unused rooms that show signs of having been occupied at some earlier period — faded curtains, an old bedframe, a water stain on the ceiling from a leak long since repaired. Ashgrove remembers residents Eris never met.

X. Atmosphere Notes
What Ashgrove feels like:
It is not a prison and it is not a home. It is something in between — a place that could become either depending on who you are and how long you stay. The stone holds warmth in the evenings and cold in the mornings. The timber creaks in wind. The library smells of old paper and mineral damp. The courtyard gravel announces every arrival. The wards shimmer twice a day like a reminder written in light.
For Eris: Ashgrove is the only place he does not perform. The study, the library, the hallway at 3am — these are the spaces where the instrument can be set down, briefly, before the next day's work picks it up again. He chose this place because it was defensible and discreet. He stayed because it was the closest thing to peace his life has produced.
For Aurelie: Ashgrove is a cage that keeps trying to feel like a house. The bath is hot. The food is good. The library is extraordinary. The balcony catches morning light. And none of it is hers. She is making it hers anyway — the stables, the library, the green room, the lesser fae who are starting to see her — and the act of claiming space in a place that was designed to contain her is its own quiet rebellion.
For Prince: Ashgrove is the first place since hatching where his magic can exist at full volume without endangering Aurelie. The wards hum at a frequency he recognizes. He is, for the first time, not hiding. The lodge is too small for what he is becoming, but for now, it holds him. He sleeps on Aurelie's bed and his tail hangs off the end and the golden blade clicks against the floor when he dreams.
