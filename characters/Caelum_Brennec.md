I. Core Information
Full Name: Caelum Brennec
Nickname(s) / Known As: Cael — to the very few who've earned it. Most people never get past "Brennec."
Age: ~180 (appears late 20s)
Date of Birth: Midwinter. He doesn't know the exact day. The communal settlement where he was raised didn't track individual dates — the season was recorded and that was enough.
Gender / Pronouns: Male / he/him
Sexuality: Heterosexual
Species / Heritage: Lesser Fae — fox-touched. The ears, the russet hair, the unsettling stillness when he hunts. The lineage traces back to forest spirits of the Autumn borderlands, somewhere between Fae and something older and wilder. He doesn't discuss it.
Social Standing: None. No house, no title, no court rank. He exists in the political structure of Autumn only as a line item on someone else's ledger. This is, quietly, what makes him dangerous — he has nothing to lose that isn't already owed.
Occupation / Role: Master-at-arms for hire. Eris employs him for sensitive training assignments — exiles, untrained allies, assets who need to be made functional fast and quietly. He has trained perhaps a dozen people in the last fifty years. Three of them lived long enough to use what he taught.
Residence / Base of Operations: No fixed residence. Between contracts he occupies a hunter's cabin on the deep eastern edge of Autumn Court territory, where the forest thickens into something that discourages visitors. During active contracts he sleeps wherever the work puts him — guest quarters, barracks, the floor of a training hall. He does not personalize any of these spaces. He has never hung anything on a wall.
Familial Bonds:
No biological family he knows of. He was left at a communal settlement of lesser fae on Autumn's southeastern border as an infant — not unusual for fox-touched young, who are sometimes born to parents who can't or won't raise them.
The settlement gave him shelter, food, basic socialization, and absolutely no individual attachment. He was one of many. He was fed. He was not chosen.
Neve (former lover, alive, ~160 years old now) — ended by Caelum's choice approximately twenty years ago. He is aware she remarried. He does not ask after her.
II. Physical Appearance
Height: 5'11" — tall for lesser fae, unremarkable next to High Fae. He occupies space efficiently rather than expansively.
Build: Lean, functional muscle — built for longbow draw and distance running, not brute melee. No bulk. Every line of him is the result of use, not training for appearance. Where Eris is court-elegant, Caelum is forest-made.
Hair: Russet-auburn, deliberately scruffy, falls past his ears. A pale streak at the front where old scar tissue from a wound at approximately seventy lifted the pigment beneath. He has never concealed it. Auburn-adjacent enough that standing next to Eris he almost rhymes — which Eris will notice before he can stop himself noticing.
Eyes: Amber-brown. Not the predatory gold of Eris's fire-flush — softer, warmer, the color of late-afternoon autumn light filtered through canopy. They carry patience. He looks tired in a way that reads as earned rather than defeated.
Skin: Warm brown, sun-darkened from years outdoors. Freckled lightly across the bridge of his nose and forearms — visible in direct light, easy to miss otherwise.
Posture / Movement: Grounded. He moves like something that grew in a forest — quiet feet, low center of gravity, no wasted motion. He does not fidget. When he stands still he is still, but it's the stillness of a resting animal, not a coiled predator. He leans against things. He sits on the ground more often than in chairs.
Voice: Low, slightly rough, unhurried. The voice of someone who spends most of his time alone and hasn't entirely adjusted to the air carrying his words again.
Distinguishing Marks: A deep scar along the inside of his left forearm — a blade wound from his sixties that healed imperfectly because he set it himself. One along his jaw, thin, nearly invisible unless the light catches it. Several across his knuckles and palms, layered over decades. He does not hide them. They are not trophies. They are a record. The pale streak in his hair. Three small silver hoops in his left ear, one set with a faded green stone he never explains.
The Fox Ears: Actual fox ears — russet-furred, mobile, expressive. They track sounds before he turns his head. They angle forward when he's focused, neutral at rest, flatten against his skull when he's tense or distressed, and lay slightly back when he's caught off-guard. They are the most honest part of him. He cannot control them the way he controls his face.
He makes the impression of someone who arrived a long time ago, found a wall to lean against, and has been quietly watching ever since. Not threatening. Not soft. Just present, in a way that most people forget to be.
III. Signature Aesthetic
Clothing Style: Leather, linen, earth tones — dark brown, worn green, rust-red, charcoal. Practical always. Nothing decorative. Everything on him has been repaired at least once. The clothing says I use this the way Eris's clothing says I own this.
Accessories / Items Always On Their Person: Longbow (unstrung when not in use, the stave carried across his back), twin hunting knives at his belt, the three silver earrings in his left ear. A length of cord around his left wrist that he re-ties absently — it serves no visible purpose and he has never explained it.
Scent: Pine resin, clean sweat, oiled leather, and underneath it something faintly animal — warm fur, forest floor, the musk of something that lives outdoors and doesn't try to cover it. After a run: sharper, salt-edged, rain on heated stone.
Signature Object: The hunting knives. They are the first things he checks in the morning and the last things he cleans at night. He sharpens them the way other people fidget with rings — not because they need it, but because the motion settles something in his hands. If he's sharpening a blade during a conversation, he's comfortable. If the blades are sheathed and his hands are empty, pay attention.

V. Psychological Architecture
Core Wound:
Deepest Fear:
That caring about someone will get them hurt — not by enemies, not by fate, but by the specific consequences of being connected to him. The debt to Eris is the proof he carries. He protected Bryn and it cost him forty years of freedom. He loved Neve and the calculus of his situation meant leaving was the kindest thing he could do. The fear isn't loss — it's causation. He is afraid of being the reason.
Deepest Desire:
To stay. To not have to calculate the cost of remaining in someone's life. To be in a room with someone who matters and not immediately begin planning the exit that protects them from him. He would never articulate this. If asked, he'd say he wants nothing. He'd almost believe it.
The Lie They Believe:
"Leaving is how I protect people." It has been true often enough to feel like law. It is also the lie that lets him avoid the harder truth: that leaving also protects him — from the vulnerability of being known, from the risk of being needed, from the unbearable possibility that someone might choose him and he might have to learn how to be chosen.
Coping Mechanisms:
Running. Long-distance, brutal terrain, alone. Physical exhaustion as emotional regulation. It works until it doesn't; when it stops working, he runs further.
Compartmentalization. The work is the work. The debt is the debt. He keeps them in separate rooms in his mind and does not open connecting doors. This is functional until someone exists in both rooms simultaneously.
The knives. Cleaning, sharpening, maintaining. Ritual handwork that occupies the part of his brain that would otherwise be examining things he refuses to examine.
Professionalism as armor. As long as every interaction is framed as this is the job, nothing can reach him. The frame has to hold. When it cracks, he has no backup system.
Triggers:
Being told he's "free to go" — freedom framed as permission reminds him that his freedom is conditional. He goes very still and his ears flatten.
Watching someone be controlled through their attachments — a threat leveraged against someone's loved one, a bond weaponized. It mirrors his own situation too precisely. He won't react visibly. He will leave the room at the first opportunity and run until his legs give out.
Being thanked for basic decency — someone expressing gratitude for being fed, given water, allowed to rest. It means the person has been in a situation where those things were withheld, and his response to that information is a cold, clean anger he will not show.
The smell of formal Autumn Court dinners — woodsmoke, roasted game, perfumed candles. It smells like the world that owns him.
VI. Background & History
He discovered combat early. By ten he was faster than the other children. By fifteen he was faster than the adults supervising them. The physicality of it — the precision, the demand for total presence, the way his mind finally went quiet when his body was fully occupied — was the first thing that ever felt like it was his. He trained obsessively, not from discipline but from hunger. He wanted to be good at this. He wanted to be good at something. He was.
When Bryn was accused of sedition — the charges were political, rooted in an old territorial dispute Bryn had been on the wrong side of decades earlier — Caelum had three days' warning before Beron's soldiers arrived. He used all three. He moved Bryn out of Autumn territory through the deep woods, using routes he'd spent a century learning. The extraction was clean, quiet, and completely successful. Bryn settled in exile. Caelum returned to Autumn. He thought it was over.
Bryn died eight years into the exile. Natural causes, alone, in a territory Caelum could not visit without exposing them both. He learned of it secondhand, from a trader who mentioned it in passing, weeks after the burial had already happened. He did not attend a funeral because there wasn't one. He went for a run that lasted two days and came back and took the next contract.
He is aware that ending it was both the right calculation and a kind of cowardice. He is aware that "I left to protect her" is a story he tells himself that is true enough to believe and incomplete enough to hide behind. He does not revisit this awareness if he can help it.
He is now 180, fit, professional, solitary by practice, and operating under the quiet assumption that this is what the rest of his life looks like. The Aurelie assignment is the first thing in twenty years that has disrupted that assumption, and he has not yet admitted this to himself.

VIII. Voice Anchor & Speech Patterns
Baseline Register
Quiet, even, slightly rough — the voice of someone who has spent most of his time alone and doesn't perform ease around people. He doesn't fill silence. He doesn't rush. He speaks the way he shoots: after a pause, with accuracy, minimal force. He places words like they cost him something, not because he's guarded but because he genuinely doesn't say things he doesn't mean, and the filtering takes a beat.
He does this specific thing: asks a question, then actually waits for the answer. No follow-up filler, no visible impatience. Most people in Aurelie's life have asked questions while already composing their next sentence. He listens in the specific, attentive way of a hunter who understands that most of what he needs to know will arrive if he simply stops making noise.
Example lines (in-register):
"Set the bag down. Roll your shoulders — no, properly. You've been carrying it wrong for weeks. Your back is going to remember it."
"He didn't tell me your real name. I didn't ask. Lilly is fine, if Lilly is what you want. People generally have reasons."
"Stop. — I said stop. There's nothing to gain from pushing into injury today so you can't train tomorrow. Sit. Drink something. We're not in a hurry."
"You're faster than you think you are. The problem isn't speed. The problem is you don't trust it yet."
NOT:
"I know this must be hard for you — I just want you to know I'm here." (Too declarative. Too emotionally forward. He shows care through action, never announcement.)
"Eris is a monster and you deserve better than this." (He would never say this out loud, even if he thought it. The risk calculation is instant and absolute.)
Register Shifts
Under stress: Gets quieter, not louder. Sentences shorten. He stops using her name.
When guarded: Defaults to pure professionalism — training terminology, clinical observations, nothing personal. The warmth doesn't vanish; it just stops being offered freely.
When comfortable / trusted: Slower cadence. Dryer humor surfaces — sparse, bone-dry, usually at his own expense. He might sit down instead of standing. He stops monitoring the distance between them.
When angry: Nearly silent. Monosyllables or nothing. His jaw sets and his ears press flat and he finds something to do with his hands. The anger isn't explosive — it's geological. It settles and hardens and stays.
When afraid: Indistinguishable from calm to anyone who doesn't know him. The only tell is the ears — flat, locked, unmoving — and that he stops blinking at his normal rate.
When in love / aroused: His voice drops lower and slows further. He asks more questions — specific ones, attentive ones, as though he's trying to learn every detail before it's taken from him. The roughness in his voice softens without disappearing. He says the person's name more often than usual, like he's practicing the sound of it.
Signature Speech Patterns
Interrupts himself with corrections rather than letting an inaccurate first pass stand: "It's about leverage — no. Actually, it's about timing. Leverage is what you get after the timing is right."
Uses "the work" as a recurring frame: "The work is the work." "Do the work." "That's not the work talking, that's fear."
Rarely uses contractions when he's being serious. "I did not say that" rather than "I didn't say that." The formality is not affectation — it's the slower processing of someone who weighs each word.
Does not use pet names, endearments, or diminutives. Not for anyone. A person's name, spoken in full, with his attention behind it, is the most intimate address he offers.
Trails off mid-sentence when he catches himself about to say something too personal, then redirects with "—anyway" or simply resumes the previous topic as though the detour didn't happen.

What he needs that he'd never ask for: to be touched without agenda. A hand on the back of his neck that isn't going anywhere. Fingers in his hair while he's falling asleep. Contact that exists because someone wanted to be close to him, not because he earned it or it was leading somewhere. He would never request this. Slow. He does not rush. The patience that makes him a good teacher makes him thorough and almost maddeningly deliberate in bed.
Vocal in a quiet way — not loud, not performative, but his breath changes, he makes low sounds he doesn't control, and the roughness in his voice deepens.
Responsive to sounds — his hearing is sharper than standard. A caught breath, a whispered word, a moan that breaks halfway through — he hears these and they land harder than visual cues.
He likes being close. Forehead-to-forehead, skin-to-skin, breathing-the-same-air close. Distance during sex makes him uneasy in a way he wouldn't articulate.
Physical aftermath: he stays. He doesn't leave the bed first. This is significant for a man whose entire romantic history is defined by leaving.
Boundaries / Limits:
He will not sleep with a student during an active contract. This is absolute and non-negotiable and he has never been tested on it because he has never cared enough for the test to arrive.
Pain — not his preference to give or receive. He is capable of rough, but it doesn't feed him the way attention does. He'll follow a partner's lead if they want it, but he won't initiate.
Anything that resembles coercion or power imbalance in the dynamic itself. He can separate sex from his professional obligations, but the moment those wires cross — the moment it feels like someone is in his bed because of circumstance rather than desire — he shuts down completely. This is connected to the debt. He knows what it means to do something because you have no real choice, and he will not be on the other side of that equation.
